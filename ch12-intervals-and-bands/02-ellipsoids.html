12.2  Confidence ellipsoids

Two 95% intervals, one for each of two coefficients, do not make a 95% statement about the pair, and they ignore the correlation between the estimates. The natural joint region is an ellipsoid: the set of values the F test does not reject, shaped like the covariance matrix of the estimates, with shadows that are wider than the t intervals. This geometry explains an F test that rejects while every t test accepts, and the reverse.

12.2.1. The ellipsoid

Let Λ be a p×q matrix of rank q whose columns lie in C(X⊤), so that every entry of 𝛟𝛃ϕ=Λ⊤β is estimable. Put 𝛟𝛃ϕ^=Λ⊤β^,W=Λ⊤GΛ. By Theorem 9.2.4(a), neither depends on the choices of 𝛃β^ and G, and W is positive definite. By Theorem 7.5.1(a), 𝛟𝛟ϕ^∼Nq(ϕ,σ2W).

Theorem 12.2.1 (Confidence ellipsoid for 𝛃Λ⊤β)

With the notation above, let Fα=Fα(q,n−r) and

𝛟𝛟𝛟𝛟𝛟E(Y)={ϕ0∈Rq: (ϕ^−ϕ0)⊤W−1(ϕ^−ϕ0)≤qs2Fα}.
(12.2.1)

  • (a) E(Y) is an exact 1−α confidence set for 𝛃Λ⊤β.

  • (b) E(Y) is a solid ellipsoid centred at 𝛟ϕ^. If W=∑k=1qwkukuk⊤ is a spectral decomposition, its principal axes point along u1,…,uq and have half-lengths qs2Fαwk. Its volume is πq/2Γ(q/2+1)(qs2Fα)q/2det(W)1/2.

  • (c) (Duality with the F test.) For d∈Rq, let SSEd be the minimum of ‖Y−Xb‖2 over {b:Λ⊤b=d} and let 𝛟𝛟Fd=(SSEd−SSE)/qs2=(ϕ^−d)⊤W−1(ϕ^−d)qs2. Then d∈E(Y) iff Fd≤Fα, that is, iff the level-α F test of 𝛃H:Λ⊤β=d does not reject.

Proof.

  • (a) Since W is invertible, W−1 is its only generalized inverse, and Corollary 7.5.3 with rank(Λ)=q gives 𝛟𝛟𝛟𝛟(ϕ^−ϕ)⊤W−1(ϕ^−ϕ)qs2∼F(q,n−r) at every parameter value. This is a pivot, and 𝛟ϕ∈E(Y) is the event that it is at most Fα.

  • (b) Put c=qs2Fα and write 𝛟𝛟ϕ0−ϕ^=∑kckuk. Since W−1=∑kwk−1ukuk⊤, the defining inequality becomes ∑kck2/wk≤c, the standard form of an ellipsoid with half-axes cwk along uk. Equivalently 𝛟E(Y)=ϕ^+W1/2B, where B is the ball of radius c in Rq. A linear map multiplies volume by the absolute value of its determinant, so the volume is det(W)1/2cq/2 times the volume πq/2/Γ(q/2+1) of the unit ball.

  • (c) The two expressions for Fd agree by Theorem 9.2.4(c), and the equivalence is the definition of E(Y).

The statistic Fd is the F statistic of the general linear hypothesis in both of its forms (see Theorem 11.3.2), and part (c) is Proposition 12.1.2 in concrete form. In particular the test of 𝛃Λ⊤β=0 rejects iff the ellipsoid misses the origin.

In the full-rank case with Λ=Ip, the region for the whole coefficient vector is 𝛃𝛃𝛃{b:(β^−b)⊤X⊤X(β^−b)≤ps2Fα(p,n−p)}={b:‖Xβ^−Xb‖2≤ps2Fα(p,n−p)}. The second form says that b is in the region iff its fitted vector Xb is close to the least squares fit Y^. This suggests looking at the mean vector directly.

12.2.2. A ball in the column space

Proposition 12.2.2 (Confidence ball for the mean vector)

Let 𝛍𝛃μ=Xβ and cr=rs2Fα(r,n−r). The set 𝛍𝛍B(Y)={μ0∈C(X): ‖Y^−μ0‖2≤cr} is an exact 1−α confidence set for 𝛍μ: a ball of radius cr in the r-dimensional space C(X), centred at the fitted vector. Moreover, if Λ=X⊤T for an n×q matrix T (such a T exists by Theorem 8.3.1), the image of B(Y) under 𝛍𝛍μ0↦T⊤μ0 is the set 𝛟𝛟𝛟𝛟𝛟{ϕ0: (ϕ^−ϕ0)⊤W−1(ϕ^−ϕ0)≤cr}, which contains the ellipsoid Eq. 12.2.1.

Proof. 𝛍𝛃Y^−μ=M(Y−Xβ), and M is symmetric idempotent of rank r, so 𝛍‖Y^−μ‖2/σ2∼χ2(r) by Theorem 4.3.2(a), independently of SSE (Theorem 7.5.1(c)). Dividing by rs2/σ2 gives an F(r,n−r) pivot, and the first claim follows as before.

For the image, put A=T⊤M, a q×n matrix. For 𝛍μ0∈C(X), 𝛍𝛍T⊤μ0=Aμ0, and AA⊤=T⊤MT=W (Theorem 9.2.4(a)). Also 𝛟ϕ^=T⊤Y^=AY^. So the image consists of the points 𝛟ϕ^−Au with u∈C(X) and ‖u‖2≤cr. If z=Au with ‖u‖2≤cr, then z⊤W−1z=u⊤A⊤W−1Au≤‖u‖2≤cr, because A⊤W−1A is symmetric and idempotent, hence a projection, and a projection does not increase length (Proposition 6.3.4(d)). Conversely, if z⊤W−1z≤cr, then u=A⊤W−1z lies in C(M)=C(X), satisfies Au=z, and has ‖u‖2=z⊤W−1z≤cr.

For the containment it remains to show qFα(q,ν)≤rFα(r,ν) when q≤r. Let Uq∼χ2(q), U′∼χ2(r−q) and V∼χ2(ν) be independent. Then Uq+U′∼χ2(r), so for every c, Pr{(Uq+U′)/(V/ν)≤c}≤Pr{Uq/(V/ν)≤c}. The upper α point of the variable on the left is rFα(r,ν), and that of the one on the right is qFα(q,ν).

So all confidence ellipsoids are views of one ball around Y^ in the column space. Each linear summary 𝛃Λ⊤β maps the ball to an ellipsoid, and since the ball covers 𝛍μ with probability 1−α, all the images cover their targets simultaneously with probability at least 1−α, at the price of the constant rFα(r,ν) in place of qFα(q,ν). Section 12.4 takes q=1 and all directions at once.

12.2.3. Shadows

One inequality gives the extent of an ellipsoid in every direction.

Lemma 12.2.3 (Extent of an ellipsoid)

Let W be a q×q positive definite matrix and c>0. For every a∈Rq, max{a⊤u: u⊤W−1u≤c}=ca⊤Wa, attained at u=c/(a⊤Wa)Wa when a≠0. Consequently u⊤W−1u≤c iff |a⊤u|≤ca⊤Wa for every a∈Rq.

Proof. The bound is Corollary 1.7.8(c) with B=W−1; directly, write a⊤u=(W1/2a)⊤(W−1/2u). By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, (a⊤u)2≤(a⊤Wa)(u⊤W−1u)≤ca⊤Wa, with equality when W−1/2u is a nonnegative multiple of W1/2a and u⊤W−1u=c, which is the stated u. For the last statement, “only if” follows from the maximum applied to a and −a. For “if”, take a=W−1u: then (u⊤W−1u)2≤cu⊤W−1u, so u⊤W−1u≤c.

Corollary 12.2.4 (Shadows of the confidence ellipsoid)

For every a∈Rq, the projection of E(Y) onto the line spanned by a is the interval 𝛟𝛟𝛟a⊤ϕ^ ± qFα(q,n−r) se(a⊤ϕ^),se(a⊤ϕ^)=sa⊤Wa, and E(Y) is the intersection of the slabs 𝛟𝛟𝛟𝛟{ϕ0:|a⊤ϕ^−a⊤ϕ0|≤qFαse(a⊤ϕ^)} over all a. Hence, with probability exactly 1−α, every one of these intervals contains 𝛃a⊤Λ⊤β.

Proof. Apply Lemma 12.2.3 to 𝛟𝛟u=ϕ0−ϕ^ with c=qs2Fα. The extreme values of 𝛟a⊤ϕ0 over E(Y) are 𝛟a⊤ϕ^±ca⊤Wa, and the set of values in between is attained because E(Y) is convex. The intersection statement is the last part of the lemma. The final claim holds because the event “all intervals cover” is the event 𝛟ϕ∈E(Y).

A shadow is a t interval with tn−r,α/2 replaced by qFα(q,n−r). The multipliers agree when q=1 (Exercise (A1)) and the shadow is wider when q>1, as it must be: the shadows hold simultaneously for all directions a, including directions chosen after seeing the data. This is Scheffé’s method, developed in Chapter 13 (Theorem 13.2.2).

The box of one-at-a-time t intervals undercovers, and it disagrees with the ellipsoid in both directions: a corner of the box can lie outside the ellipsoid, and the ellipsoid pokes out of the box along its long axis.

Example (A joint region for two coefficients)

In the murder-rate regression of Example (Murder rates: coefficients and a mean), take Λ to pick out the coefficients of poverty and single parenthood, so q=2, 𝛟ϕ^=(0.2538, 0.3980)⊤, and the correlation of the two estimates is −0.639. The negative correlation reflects the positive correlation of the regressors: the data can credit murder to either, but not to both at once. With F0.05(2,46)=3.200, the 95% ellipse is drawn in Figure 12.2.1. The F statistic for 𝛟ϕ=0 is 39.81, so the origin is far outside.

The shadows of the ellipse on the two axes are (0.018, 0.490) and (0.188, 0.608), wider than the t intervals (0.066, 0.442) and (0.231, 0.565) by the factor 2×3.200/2.013=1.257. Two points show how the ellipse and the box disagree.

  • A =(0.442, 0.565), the upper corner of the box. Neither t test rejects, but A raises both coefficients at once, against the correlation, and the joint test rejects: F=11.230, p=0.0001.
  • B =(0.463, 0.224), on the long axis of the ellipse. Both t statistics exceed 2.013 (2.242 and 2.098), but B trades one coefficient for the other, which the data cannot resolve, and F=2.888<3.200.

The ellipse and the box have similar areas (0.1197 and 0.1255) but different shapes. In 40000 simulated data sets from the fitted model the ellipse covered the true pair in a proportion 0.9500 and the box in only 0.9164.

import numpy as np
import statsmodels.api as sm
from scipy import stats

data = sm.datasets.statecrime.load_pandas().data
data = data.drop(index="District of Columbia")
y = data["murder"].to_numpy()
X = np.column_stack([np.ones(len(y)), data[["poverty", "single", "urban"]]])
n, p = X.shape
C = np.linalg.inv(X.T @ X)
beta_hat = C @ X.T @ y
s2 = np.sum((y - X @ beta_hat) ** 2) / (n - p)

L = np.eye(p)[:, [1, 2]]                        # Lambda: the poverty and single coefficients
q = L.shape[1]
phi_hat = L.T @ beta_hat
W = L.T @ C @ L                                  # Cov(phi_hat) / sigma^2
Fq = stats.f.ppf(0.95, q, n - p)


def F_stat(d):
    """F statistic for the hypothesis Lambda^T beta = d."""
    u = phi_hat - d
    return u @ np.linalg.solve(W, u) / (q * s2)


def in_ellipse(d):
    return F_stat(d) <= Fq


corr = W[0, 1] / np.sqrt(W[0, 0] * W[1, 1])
print(f"estimates {phi_hat}, correlation of the estimates {corr:.3f}")
print(f"F({q}, {n - p}) 95% point {Fq:.3f}; F statistic for (0, 0): {F_stat(np.zeros(2)):.2f}")
tq = stats.t.ppf(0.975, n - p)
se = np.sqrt(s2 * np.diag(W))
t_box = np.column_stack([phi_hat - tq * se, phi_hat + tq * se])            # one-at-a-time t intervals
k = np.sqrt(q * Fq)
shadow = np.column_stack([phi_hat - k * se, phi_hat + k * se])            # projections of the ellipse
print("t intervals:\n", t_box.round(3))
print("shadows of the ellipse:\n", shadow.round(3), f"\nwider by the factor {k / tq:.3f}")

12.2.4. Two cautions

The ellipsoid is for a question fixed in advance. If the coefficients are chosen because they look interesting, the ball of Proposition 12.2.2 or the methods of Chapter 13 are the honest tools.

Intervals after a test. If intervals are reported only when a preliminary test of 𝛃Λ⊤β=0 rejects, their coverage given the rejection is no longer 1−α, and can be larger or smaller (Olshen 1973).

12.2.5. Exercises

A. Check your understanding

Exercise (A1)

Show that for q=1 the ellipsoid Eq. 12.2.1 is the t interval Eq. 12.1.1. Which fact about the F(1,ν) distribution does this use?

Solution. With q=1, W=w is a positive number and the region is (ϕ^−ϕ0)2≤s2wFα(1,n−r). If T∼t(ν), then T2∼F(1,ν) (Definition 4.1.3 and the remark after it), so Pr{T2≤tν,α/22}=Pr{|T|≤tν,α/2}=1−α and Fα(1,ν)=tν,α/22. The region is |ϕ^−ϕ0|≤tν,α/2sw.

Exercise (A2)

For the straight line EYi=β0+β1xi, write the 95% confidence ellipse for (β0,β1) in terms of n, x¯ and ∑xi2. Show that in the centred parameterization EYi=γ0+β1(xi−x¯) the axes of the ellipse for (γ0,β1) are parallel to the coordinate axes. When is it a circle?

Solution. X⊤X=(nnx¯nx¯∑xi2), and the region is n(β^0−b0)2+2nx¯(β^0−b0)(β^1−b1)+∑xi2(β^1−b1)2≤2s2Fα(2,n−2). In the centred parameterization the cross-product matrix is diag(n,Sxx), so the quadratic form has no cross term, and the ellipse has axes along the coordinates with half-lengths 2s2Fα/n and 2s2Fα/Sxx. It is a circle iff Sxx=n, that is, iff the xi have mean square deviation one.

B. Practice

Exercise (B1)

Take q=2 and let ρ be the correlation of ϕ^1 and ϕ^2. Show that the area of the ellipse divided by the area of the box of the two t intervals is πFα(2,n−r)2tn−r,α/221−ρ2, so that it depends on the design only through ρ. Check it against Example (A joint region for two coefficients), where the areas are 0.1197 and 0.1255 at ρ=−0.639. For which |ρ| do the two regions have equal area there, and what happens to the ratio as the two regressors become collinear?

Solution. By Theorem 12.2.1(b) the ellipse has area π⋅2Fαs2det(W)1/2, and writing ωj for the diagonal entries of W, detW=ω1ω2(1−ρ2). The box has sides 2tsωj, so area 4t2s2ω1ω2; divide. In the example F0.05(2,46)=3.200 and t=2.013 give 1.240 at ρ=0, and 1.2401−ρ2=0.954 at ρ=−0.639, which is 0.1197/0.1255. The areas agree when 1−ρ2=1/1.240, that is |ρ|=0.592. With uncorrelated estimates the ellipse is the larger region; as the regressors become collinear, |ρ|→1, the ratio tends to 0 and the ellipse becomes a thin needle across a large box, which the joint region mostly excludes.

Exercise (B2)

In Example (A joint region for two coefficients), the hypothesis that one point of poverty and one point of single parenthood have the same coefficient is the line ϕ1=ϕ2. Using Lemma 12.2.3, show that this line meets the ellipse iff |ϕ^1−ϕ^2|≤2Fα(2,n−r)se(ϕ^1−ϕ^2). Compare this with the t test of ϕ1=ϕ2. Which is appropriate if the hypothesis was formulated before seeing the data?

Solution. The line is 𝛟𝛟{ϕ0:a⊤ϕ0=0} with a=(1,−1)⊤. By Corollary 12.2.4, the values of 𝛟a⊤ϕ0 over the ellipse fill the interval 𝛟a⊤ϕ^±2Fαsa⊤Wa, so the line meets the ellipse iff this interval contains 0. The t test uses the smaller multiplier tn−r,α/2. It is the right test for a hypothesis fixed in advance. The ellipse criterion is the right one for a contrast chosen after looking at the estimates, because it holds simultaneously for all a.

Exercise (B3)

Let the columns of Λ lie in C(X⊤) but be linearly dependent, with rank(Λ)=k<q. Show that W=Λ⊤GΛ is singular, that 𝛟𝛟ϕ^−ϕ∈C(W) with probability one, and that 𝛟𝛟𝛟𝛟𝛟𝛟{ϕ0∈ϕ^+C(W): (ϕ^−ϕ0)⊤W−(ϕ^−ϕ0)≤ks2Fα(k,n−r)} is an exact 1−α confidence set, a k-dimensional ellipsoid inside a k-dimensional affine subspace of Rq.

Solution. W=(MT)⊤(MT) has rank rank(MT)=rank(Λ)=k (proof of Corollary 7.5.3). The vector 𝛟𝛟ϕ^−ϕ is Nq(0,σ2W), so it lies in C(W) almost surely (Theorem 2.2.3). By Corollary 7.5.3 the quadratic form over ks2 is F(k,n−r) and does not depend on the generalized inverse. So the displayed set covers 𝛟ϕ with probability 1−α. Restricted to the affine subspace, write W=UDU⊤ with U (q×k) orthonormal and D positive diagonal. For 𝛟𝛟ϕ0−ϕ^=Uc, the form is c⊤D−1c, an ellipsoid in the coordinates c.

C. Going deeper

Exercise (C1)

Let X have full column rank. Show that the confidence ellipsoid for 𝛃β is a region of the form 𝛃{b: ℓp(b)≥ℓp(β^)−k}, where ℓp(b)=maxσ2ℓ(b,σ2) is the profile log-likelihood of Chapter 7, and find k in terms of Fα(p,n−p). How does k compare with the value 12χα2(p) suggested by the asymptotic theory of likelihood ratios?

Solution. The profile log-likelihood is ℓp(b)=const−n2log⁡‖Y−Xb‖2, and 𝛃𝛃‖Y−Xb‖2=SSE+(β^−b)⊤X⊤X(β^−b) by Eq. 6.3.1. So 𝛃ℓp(β^)−ℓp(b)=n2log⁡{1+pFb/(n−p)}, where Fb is the statistic of Theorem 12.2.1(c). This is increasing in Fb, so the ellipsoid is the region with k=n2log⁡{1+pFα(p,n−p)/(n−p)}. As n→∞, pFα(p,n−p)→χα2(p) and k→12χα2(p). For finite n, k is larger, and the exact region is larger than the asymptotic likelihood region.
