12.4  Simultaneous bands for the regression line and surface

A fitted line is usually drawn inside a shaded region. If the region is made of pointwise intervals, each vertical slice covers the true mean with probability 1−α, but the region does not cover the whole true line with that probability. A reader who asks “which lines are consistent with the data?” needs a simultaneous band, and for a linear model the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives one with exact coverage.

12.4.1. Pointwise is not simultaneous

Definition 12.4.1 (Simultaneous confidence band)

Let S⊆C(X⊤) be a set of regressor vectors. Random functions ℓ(x)=ℓ(x;Y) and u(x)=u(x;Y) form a 1−α simultaneous confidence band for the regression function over S if 𝛃𝛃Prβ,σ2{ℓ(x)≤x⊤β≤u(x)  for every x∈S} ≥ 1−α for every 𝛃(β,σ2).

Pointwise t intervals fail this: for the stack loss line of Example (A band for the stack loss line), the pointwise 95% intervals cover the true line over the observed range in only 0.8705 of simulated samples.

12.4.2. The Working–Hotelling band

Theorem 12.4.1 (Working–Hotelling band)

Let L be a linear subspace of C(X⊤) of dimension k≥1, and let wk=kFα(k,n−r). Then

𝛃𝛃Pr{|x⊤β^−x⊤β|≤wksx⊤Gx  for all x∈L}=1−α.
(12.4.1)

In particular:

  • (a) with L=C(X⊤) and k=r, the functions 𝛃x⊤β^±wrsx⊤Gx form an exact 1−α simultaneous band for the regression surface over all estimable x;

  • (b) for a straight line EYi=β0+β1xi with the xi not all equal, the band β^0+β^1x ± 2Fα(2,n−2) s1n+(x−x¯)2Sxx,x∈R, contains the true line β0+β1x for every real x with probability exactly 1−α.

Proof. Step 1: from L to a subspace of C(X). The map u↦X⊤u is one to one on C(X), since X⊤u=0 with u∈C(X) forces u∈C(X)∩C(X)⊥={0}. It maps C(X) onto C(X⊤), because 𝛒𝛒X⊤ρ=X⊤Mρ. So U={u∈C(X):X⊤u∈L} is a subspace of C(X) of dimension k, and every x∈L is X⊤u for exactly one u∈U. For such a pair, 𝛃𝛃𝛃𝛃𝛃x⊤β^−x⊤β=u⊤(Xβ^−Xβ)=u⊤M(Y−Xβ)=u⊤v,x⊤Gx=u⊤XGX⊤u=u⊤Mu=‖u‖2, where 𝛃v=PU(Y−Xβ). The middle equality uses 𝛃Xβ^=MY and MX=X. The last step of the first chain uses u=Mu=PUu, and the second chain uses Theorem 6.3.7.

Step 2: the supremum. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, for u∈U, u≠0, 𝛃𝛃(x⊤β^−x⊤β)2x⊤Gx=(u⊤v)2‖u‖2≤‖v‖2, with equality at u=v when v≠0, and v∈U. So the supremum of the left side over x∈L∖{0} equals ‖v‖2 and is attained.

Step 3: its distribution. 𝛃Y−Xβ∼Nn(0,σ2I), and PU is symmetric idempotent of rank k, so ‖v‖2/σ2∼χ2(k) (Theorem 4.3.2(a)). Since U⊆C(X), PU=PUM, so v is a function of MY and is independent of SSE (Theorem 7.5.1(c)). Hence ‖v‖2/(ks2)∼F(k,n−r) (Definition 4.1.2), and the event in Eq. 12.4.1, which is ‖v‖2≤wk2s2 by Step 2, has probability 1−α. (At x=0 both sides of the inequality are zero.)

Part (a) is the case L=C(X⊤), U=C(X).

Part (b). Here r=k=2 and L=R2, but the band uses only the vectors x=(1,x)⊤. The ratio in Step 2 is unchanged when x is multiplied by a nonzero scalar, so its supremum over {(1,x)⊤:x∈R} equals its supremum over all x=(a,b)⊤ with a≠0. By Step 2 the supremum over all of R2 is attained at x∗=X⊤v, whose first coordinate is 1⊤v. The random variable 1⊤v is normal with variance σ2‖PU1‖2=σ2n>0, because 1∈C(X)=U. So with probability one x∗ has a nonzero first coordinate, the two suprema are equal, and the band event has the probability computed in Step 3. The formula for x⊤Gx is Eq. 5.5.2 divided by σ2.

The proof has one idea: every linear function of the fitted mean is an inner product with v, and the worst case over all directions is ‖v‖. With L=C(X⊤) the band holds exactly when the confidence ball of Proposition 12.2.2 contains 𝛃Xβ: the band is that ball, viewed one linear functional at a time. Applied to general families of estimable functions, the argument gives Scheffé’s intervals, which Chapter 13 develops (Theorem 13.2.2) and compares with Bonferroni and Tukey.

The straight-line band is hyperbolic, narrowest at x¯. Although x varies along a line, the multiplier uses k=2, because the band must hold for intercept and slope together.

12.4.3. How much wider?

The band is wider than the pointwise intervals by the factor wk/tn−r,α/2, the same at every x. The multipliers wk=kF0.05(k,ν) for 95% coverage are:

k ν=5 ν=10 ν=20 ν=60 ν=∞
1 2.571 2.228 2.086 2.000 1.960
2 3.402 2.865 2.643 2.510 2.448
3 4.028 3.335 3.049 2.876 2.795
5 5.025 4.078 3.682 3.441 3.327
10 6.881 5.457 4.845 4.464 4.279

The row k=1 is the t multiplier; the column ν=∞ is χ0.052(k), for known σ. For a straight line the band is about a quarter wider than the pointwise intervals, for ten coefficients more than twice as wide: for large k simultaneity costs roughly a factor k.

Example (A band for the stack loss line)

Regress stack loss on air flow for the 21 days of Brownlee’s plant data (Example (Stack loss)), ignoring the other two regressors. The fitted line is −44.132+1.0203x with s=4.098, x¯=60.43 and Sxx=1681.1. The pointwise multiplier is t19,0.025=2.093, and the Working–Hotelling multiplier is 2F0.05(2,19)=2.654, larger by the factor 1.268. Figure 12.4.1 shows the data, the band, the pointwise intervals, and the 95% prediction intervals of Section 12.3.

Near the centre, at x=60, the fitted mean is 17.09, and the half-widths are 1.87 (pointwise), 2.38 (band) and 8.78 (prediction). At x=80, the edge of the data, the fitted mean is 37.49 and the half-widths are 4.50, 5.71 and 9.69.

In 40000 data sets simulated from the fitted line, the band contained the true line over the whole real line in a proportion 0.9500 of the samples and over the observed range 50≤x≤80 in 0.9537. The pointwise intervals covered at x=60 in 0.9517 of the samples, but covered the line over the whole observed range in only 0.8705.

import numpy as np
import statsmodels.api as sm
from scipy import stats

data = sm.datasets.stackloss.load_pandas().data
x = data["AIRFLOW"].to_numpy()
y = data["STACKLOSS"].to_numpy()
n = len(y)
X = np.column_stack([np.ones(n), x])
C = np.linalg.inv(X.T @ X)
beta_hat = C @ X.T @ y
s = np.sqrt(np.sum((y - X @ beta_hat) ** 2) / (n - 2))

grid = np.linspace(45, 85, 401)
Z = np.column_stack([np.ones_like(grid), grid])
fit = Z @ beta_hat
se_mean = s * np.sqrt(np.einsum("ij,jk,ik->i", Z, C, Z))   # s * sqrt(1/n + (x - xbar)^2 / Sxx)
t_pt = stats.t.ppf(0.975, n - 2)                            # pointwise multiplier
w_wh = np.sqrt(2 * stats.f.ppf(0.95, 2, n - 2))             # Working-Hotelling multiplier
pointwise = (fit - t_pt * se_mean, fit + t_pt * se_mean)
band = (fit - w_wh * se_mean, fit + w_wh * se_mean)
se_new = np.sqrt(s**2 + se_mean**2)
prediction = (fit - t_pt * se_new, fit + t_pt * se_new)
print(f"slope {beta_hat[1]:.4f}, s = {s:.3f}; multipliers: t {t_pt:.3f}, Working-Hotelling {w_wh:.3f}")

12.4.4. Bands over part of the space, and other shapes

The band holds over the whole line. If only x∈[a,b] matters it is conservative (the simulated 0.9537), and an exact band over [a,b] uses a smaller multiplier (Wynn and Bloomfield 1971). The hyperbolic shape is also a choice: bands of constant width, or with straight edges, come from replacing the Euclidean norm in Step 2 by another norm (Gafarian 1964; Liu 2010). And if only some regressors vary, L is smaller, and so is k.

12.4.5. Exercises

A. Check your understanding

Exercise (A1)

For a straight line with many residual degrees of freedom, compute the ratio of the Working–Hotelling multiplier to the pointwise one at α=0.05. How does the ratio change at α=0.01?

Solution. As ν→∞, 2Fα(2,ν)→χα2(2)=−2log⁡α, since χ2(2) is exponential with mean 2. At α=0.05 the ratio is 2log⁡20/z0.025=2.448/1.960=1.249. At α=0.01 it is 2log⁡100/z0.005=3.035/2.576=1.178. The relative cost of simultaneity falls as the level becomes more demanding. As α→0, both squared multipliers, zα/22 and χα2(2)=2log⁡(1/α), grow like 2log⁡(1/α)+O(log⁡log⁡(1/α)), so the ratio of the multipliers tends to 1.

Exercise (A2)

For the line through the origin EYi=βxi, show that a simultaneous band for βx over all real x is β^x±tn−1,α/2s|x|/∑xi2, with no widening at all. Explain this with the dimension k of Theorem 12.4.1.

Solution. Here C(X⊤)=R, so k=1 and w1=tn−1,α/2. Directly: the band holds at every x iff it holds at x=1, since both sides scale with |x|.

B. Practice

Exercise (B1)

For the straight line, show that the Working–Hotelling band contains the line b0+b1x for every x iff (b0,b1) lies in the 1−α confidence ellipse for (β0,β1) of Theorem 12.2.1. Deduce that the band is the envelope of all lines whose coefficients lie in the ellipse.

Solution. Put u=(β^0−b0,β^1−b1)⊤ and W=(X⊤X)−1. The line b0+b1x is inside the band at x iff |(1,x)u|≤w2s(1,x)W(1,x)⊤. By scale invariance this holds for all x iff it holds for all a=(a1,a2)⊤ with a1≠0, and by continuity iff it holds for all a. By Lemma 12.2.3 that is u⊤W−1u≤w22s2, which is the ellipse. At each x, the upper edge of the band is the maximum of b0+b1x over the ellipse (Corollary 12.2.4), so the band is the envelope.

Exercise (B2)

For quadratic regression EYi=β0+β1xi+β2xi2, the natural band over all x uses x=(1,x,x2)⊤, a curve in R3 whose directions do not fill R3. Show that the band with multiplier w3 has coverage at least 1−α over all x, and argue that its coverage is strictly larger than 1−α. (Hint: when is the maximizing direction X⊤v of Step 2 a multiple of some (1,x,x2)⊤?)

Solution. The band event over all x∈R3 has probability 1−α and is contained in the event over the curve, so the coverage is at least 1−α. Write S=‖v‖2 and Sc for the supremum of the ratio of Step 2 over the curve. The ratio depends on v only through S and the direction v/‖v‖, so Sc=ρS, where ρ≤1 is a continuous function of the direction alone. Equality ρ=1 requires either that X⊤v be a multiple of some (1,x,x2)⊤, that is, its coordinates (c0,c1,c2) satisfy c0c2=c12, or that X⊤v be a multiple of (0,0,1)⊤, the limit of (1,x,x2)⊤/x2 as x→∞, where the supremum is approached but not attained. Both are events of probability zero. For a spherical normal vector in U, the length and the direction are independent, and both are independent of s2. So the coverage over the curve exceeds 1−α by Pr{w32s2<S≤w32s2/ρ}, which is positive because S/σ2 has a positive density on (0,∞) and ρ<1 almost surely.

C. Going deeper

Exercise (C1)

A single new observation 𝛃Y0=x0⊤β+ε0 will be taken at a regressor vector x0∈C(X⊤) that is not known in advance. Show that the band 𝛃x⊤β^±(r+1)Fα(r+1,n−r)s1+x⊤Gx, x∈C(X⊤), contains Y0 with probability at least 1−α, whatever x0 turns out to be. (Hint: apply Step 2 of the proof to the (r+1)-dimensional vector (v,ε0).)

Solution. For x=X⊤u with u∈C(X), 𝛃Y0−x⊤β^=ε0−u⊤v, with 𝛃v=M(Y−Xβ), and 1+x⊤Gx=1+‖u‖2. By Cauchy–Schwarz in C(X)×R, (ε0−u⊤v)2≤(1+‖u‖2)(ε02+‖v‖2). The variable (ε02+‖v‖2)/σ2 is χ2(r+1), independent of SSE, so (ε02+‖v‖2)/((r+1)s2)∼F(r+1,n−r), and on the event that it is at most Fα(r+1,n−r), which has probability 1−α, the band contains Y0 at every x, in particular at x0.

Exercise (C2)

Two straight lines are fitted in one model, with separate intercepts and slopes and a common σ2. Construct simultaneous bands for both lines with joint coverage at least 1−α in two ways: (i) Theorem 12.4.1 with L=R4, (ii) a band for each line with L=R2 at level α/2, combined by the Bonferroni inequality. Compare the multipliers for n−r=20 and explain which is better and why.
