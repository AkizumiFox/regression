12.6  Bayesian thread II: credible intervals and predictive distributions

Section 7.6 derived the posterior distribution of 𝛃(β,σ2) under the conjugate normal-inverse-gamma prior, and showed that the flat prior 𝛃π(β,σ2)∝1/σ2 reproduces least squares. Here the posterior is summarized by credible regions and used to predict. In the conjugate model these have the shapes of the frequentist sets, and under the flat prior they coincide with them. We end by asking how often a credible interval covers in repeated sampling.

12.6.1. Credible sets

Definition 12.6.1 (Credible set)

Let θ be a function of the parameters and let π(⋅∣y) be its posterior distribution. A set C(y) with Pr{θ∈C(y)∣y}≥1−α is a 1−α credible set for θ. If the posterior has a density π(θ∣y), a set of the form {θ:π(θ∣y)≥k} with posterior probability 1−α is a highest posterior density (HPD) set.

Here the statement concerns the realized set. An HPD set has the smallest volume among sets with its posterior probability. We need three facts about the multivariate t distribution of Definition 7.6.2.

Lemma 12.6.1 (Multivariate t)

Let u∼tν(m,S) in Rp, with S positive definite.

  • (a) If A is q×p of rank q, then Au∼tν(Am,ASA⊤).

  • (b) (u−m)⊤S−1(u−m)/p∼F(p,ν).

  • (c) u has density proportional to {1+(u−m)⊤S−1(u−m)/ν}−(ν+p)/2.

Proof. By Definition 7.6.2, u=m+Z/W/ν with Z∼Np(0,S) and W∼χ2(ν) independent.

  • (a) Au=Am+AZ/W/ν, and AZ∼Nq(0,ASA⊤) (Theorem 3.3.1) is independent of W, with ASA⊤ positive definite.

  • (b) (u−m)⊤S−1(u−m)=Z⊤S−1Z/(W/ν), and Z⊤S−1Z∼χ2(p) independently of W (Corollary 4.3.3(a)). Divide by p and use Definition 4.1.2.

  • (c) Given W=w, u∼Np(m,(ν/w)S). Write D=(u−m)⊤S−1(u−m). Integrating the normal density (Theorem 3.2.2) against the χ2(ν) density of W, and dropping factors free of u, ∫0∞wp/2e−wD/(2ν)wν/2−1e−w/2dw=∫0∞w(ν+p)/2−1e−w(1+D/ν)/2dw∝(1+D/ν)−(ν+p)/2, by the substitution w′=w(1+D/ν).

12.6.2. Credible sets and predictive distributions in the conjugate model

Theorem 12.6.2 (Credible sets and posterior predictive distribution)

Let the prior be NIG(m0,V0,a0,b0), so that by Theorem 7.6.2 the posterior is NIG(mn,Vn,an,bn). Put ν=2an and τ2=bn/an.

  • (a) For 𝛌λ≠0, the interval 𝛌𝛌𝛌λ⊤mn ± tν,α/2τλ⊤Vnλ is the 1−α HPD credible interval for 𝛌𝛃λ⊤β; it is also equal-tailed.

  • (b) For a p×q matrix Λ of rank q, the set 𝛟𝛟𝛟{ϕ0: (ϕ0−Λ⊤mn)⊤(Λ⊤VnΛ)−1(ϕ0−Λ⊤mn)≤qτ2Fα(q,ν)} is the 1−α HPD credible region for 𝛃Λ⊤β.

  • (c) Let 𝛃𝛆Y0=X0β+ε0 be k future observations, with 𝛆ε0∼Nk(0,σ2Ik) independent of Y and of 𝛃β given σ2. The posterior predictive distribution of Y0 is Y0∣y ∼ tν(X0mn, τ2(Ik+X0VnX0⊤)). In particular, for one future observation at x0, the interval x0⊤mn±tν,α/2τ1+x0⊤Vnx0 has posterior predictive probability 1−α.

  • (d) [2bn/χα/22(ν), 2bn/χ1−α/22(ν)] is an equal-tailed 1−α credible interval for σ2.

Proof. By Theorem 7.6.2 and Lemma 7.6.1, 𝛃β∣y∼tν(mn,τ2Vn) and 2bn/σ2∣y∼χ2(ν).

  • (a) By Lemma 12.6.1(a) with 𝛌A=λ⊤, 𝛌𝛃𝛌𝛌𝛌λ⊤β=λ⊤mn+τλ⊤VnλT with T∼t(ν). The t(ν) density is symmetric and decreasing in |x|, so the symmetric interval is both equal-tailed and HPD.

  • (b) By Lemma 12.6.1(a), 𝛃Λ⊤β∣y∼tν(Λ⊤mn,τ2Λ⊤VnΛ). By (b) of the lemma the quadratic form divided by qτ2 is F(q,ν), so the set has posterior probability 1−α. By (c) the density is a decreasing function of the quadratic form, so the set has the form {density≥k}.

  • (c) Given σ2 and y, 𝛃β∼Np(mn,σ2Vn) and 𝛆ε0∼Nk(0,σ2Ik) independently, so Y0∼Nk(X0mn,σ2(X0VnX0⊤+Ik)) (Theorem 3.3.1). Together with σ2∣y∼IG(an,bn), this says that (Y0,σ2)∣y is NIG(X0mn,Ik+X0VnX0⊤,an,bn) (Definition 7.6.1), the matrix being positive definite. Lemma 7.6.1 gives the tν law, and (a) of the present theorem, applied to it with k=1, gives the interval.

  • (d) Pr{χ1−α/22(ν)≤2bn/σ2≤χα/22(ν)∣y}=1−α; invert the inequalities.

The formulas mirror the frequentist ones: 𝛃β^ becomes mn, G becomes Vn=(V0−1+X⊤X)−1, s2 becomes τ2, and n−r becomes ν=2a0+n, which counts every observation plus 2a0 “prior observations” about σ2. The 1 in 1+x0⊤Vnx0 is again the new error.

Corollary 12.6.3 (The flat prior reproduces the frequentist sets)

Let rank(X)=p<n, SSE>0, and let the prior be 𝛃π(β,σ2)∝1/σ2. Then the credible interval (a), the region (b), the predictive interval (c) and the interval for σ2 (d) of Theorem 12.6.2 are, respectively, the t interval of Theorem 12.1.1, the confidence ellipsoid of Theorem 12.2.1, the prediction interval of Theorem 12.3.1 and the interval of Proposition 12.1.4.

Proof. By Corollary 7.6.4 the posterior is 𝛃NIG(β^,(X⊤X)−1,(n−p)/2,SSE/2). The proof of Theorem 12.6.2 used only that the posterior is normal-inverse-gamma, so its conclusions hold with 𝛃mn=β^, Vn=(X⊤X)−1=G, ν=n−p=n−r, τ2=SSE/(n−p)=s2 and 2bn=SSE. Substituting gives the four frequentist sets.

The same set then carries two readings: “this procedure covers the fixed truth in 95% of samples” and “given these data, the parameter lies here with probability 0.95”. The agreement is special; it fails for proper priors and for most other models.

12.6.3. A worked example

Example (Predicting a day of stack loss)

Take the stack loss regression with the engineer’s prior of Example (A prior for the stack loss plant), and consider a new day with air flow 65, water temperature 23 and acid concentration 87. The posterior has an=13.5, so ν=27 and t27,0.025=2.052. By Theorem 12.6.2, the 95% credible interval for the mean stack loss at these conditions is 23.12±1.51, and the 95% posterior predictive interval for the day’s stack loss is 23.12±6.11. The least squares answers are 23.15±1.77 for the mean and 23.15±7.07 for the new day, with t17,0.025=2.110. The Bayesian intervals are shorter because the prior informs the slopes, shrinking x0⊤Vnx0, and σ2, raising the degrees of freedom from 17 to 27.

The 2.5% and 97.5% points of 400000 posterior predictive draws are 17.00 and 29.21, matching the t interval within Monte Carlo error. The simulated posterior probability of the credible ellipse of (b) for the air-flow and water-temperature coefficients is 0.9503.

import numpy as np
import statsmodels.api as sm
from scipy import stats

data = sm.datasets.stackloss.load_pandas().data
y = data["STACKLOSS"].to_numpy()
X = np.column_stack([np.ones(len(y)), data[["AIRFLOW", "WATERTEMP", "ACIDCONC"]]])
n, p = X.shape
XtX, Xty = X.T @ X, X.T @ y


def nig_posterior(m0, P0, a0, b0, y=y):
    """Conjugate update; P0 is the prior precision V0^{-1} (per unit sigma^2)."""
    Vn = np.linalg.inv(P0 + XtX)
    mn = Vn @ (P0 @ m0 + X.T @ y)
    an = a0 + n / 2
    bn = b0 + (y @ y + m0 @ P0 @ m0 - mn @ (P0 + XtX) @ mn) / 2
    return mn, Vn, an, bn


m0 = np.array([0.0, 1.0, 1.0, 0.0])                     # the prior of Chapter 7
P0 = np.linalg.inv(np.diag([100.0**2, 0.1**2, 0.2**2, 0.1**2]))
a0, b0 = 3.0, 18.0
mn, Vn, an, bn = nig_posterior(m0, P0, a0, b0)
x0 = np.array([1.0, 65.0, 23.0, 87.0])                  # a new operating condition
qb = stats.t.ppf(0.975, 2 * an)
centre = x0 @ mn
half_mean = qb * np.sqrt(bn / an * (x0 @ Vn @ x0))     # credible interval for x0^T beta
half_new = qb * np.sqrt(bn / an * (1 + x0 @ Vn @ x0))  # posterior predictive interval for Y0

beta_hat = np.linalg.solve(XtX, Xty)                   # frequentist intervals, for comparison
s2 = np.sum((y - X @ beta_hat) ** 2) / (n - p)
qf = stats.t.ppf(0.975, n - p)
h0 = x0 @ np.linalg.solve(XtX, x0)
print(f"Bayes: mean response {centre:.2f} +- {half_mean:.2f}; new day {centre:.2f} +- {half_new:.2f}")
print(f"least squares: mean response {x0 @ beta_hat:.2f} +- {qf * np.sqrt(s2 * h0):.2f}; "
      f"new day +- {qf * np.sqrt(s2 * (1 + h0)):.2f}")

12.6.4. How often does a credible interval cover?

One coverage statement holds for every proper prior.

Proposition 12.6.4 (Coverage averaged over the prior)

Let the prior π be proper, and let C(y) be a 1−α credible set for θ for every y. If 𝛃(β,σ2) is drawn from π and then Y from the model, Pr{θ∈C(Y)}≥1−α, with equality if every C(y) has posterior probability exactly 1−α. Equivalently, the frequentist coverage 𝛃Prβ,σ2{θ∈C(Y)}, averaged over π, is at least 1−α.

Proof. Under the joint distribution of parameters and data, the conditional distribution of the parameters given Y=y is the posterior. By the tower property, Pr{θ∈C(Y)}=E[Pr{θ∈C(Y)∣Y}]≥1−α. Conditioning instead on the parameters gives the second form.

The average hides variation: coverage is high where the prior is right and low where it is wrong. Consider the credible interval for the air-flow coefficient in Example (Predicting a day of stack loss), and simulate its frequentist coverage when the true air-flow coefficient is varied, the other coefficients are held at their least squares values, and σ2=s2. Figure 12.6.1 shows the result. With the prior of Chapter 7, the coverage is 0.959 when the truth is the prior mean 1, and 0.936 at 0.7 and at 1.5. It ranges from 0.877 to 0.965 over the plotted range. This prior is mild, and prior–data conflict inflates bn, which widens the interval. A prior with slope standard deviations five times smaller behaves very differently: its coverage is 1.000 at the prior mean but 0.007 at 0.7, close to the least squares estimate, and 0.000 at 1.5. Averaged over the prior of Chapter 7, the simulated coverage is 0.9512 (40000 draws), as Proposition 12.6.4 requires. The flat-prior interval is the t interval and covers exactly 95% at every parameter value.

An informative prior makes intervals shorter, and better when the prior is sound; when it is not, they are confidently wrong. The frequentist interval makes no bet and pays in length. Chapter 27 meets the same trade-off as bias against variance.

12.6.5. Exercises

A. Check your understanding

Exercise (A1)

Is the equal-tailed interval of Theorem 12.6.2(d) the HPD interval for σ2? Describe the HPD interval and explain the difference.

Solution. No. The inverse gamma density is skewed to the right, so the HPD interval [l,u], with π(l∣y)=π(u∣y), is shifted toward smaller values. It also depends on the parameterization: the HPD interval for log⁡σ2 does not transform into the one for σ2, while equal-tailed intervals do.

Exercise (A2)

For ν>2, find the posterior predictive mean and variance of a single future observation at x0. Why is the variance larger than τ2(1+x0⊤Vnx0)?

Solution. From Theorem 12.6.2(c), Y0∣y=x0⊤mn+τ1+x0⊤Vnx0T with T∼t(ν), so the mean is x0⊤mn and the variance is τ2(1+x0⊤Vnx0)ν/(ν−2). The extra factor comes from the uncertainty about σ2: given σ2 the predictive variance is σ2(1+x0⊤Vnx0), and averaging over the posterior gives E(σ2∣y)=bn/(an−1), which exceeds τ2=bn/an by the factor an/(an−1)=ν/(ν−2).

B. Practice

Exercise (B1)

Under the g-prior of Proposition 7.6.3(c) with m0=0, show that the credible interval for 𝛌𝛃λ⊤β is centred at 𝛌𝛃{g/(1+g)}λ⊤β^ and has half-width 𝛌𝛌tν,α/2τg/(1+g)λ⊤(X⊤X)−1λ. Compare it with the t interval as g→∞, paying attention to ν and τ2.

Solution. With V0=g(X⊤X)−1, Vn={g/(1+g)}(X⊤X)−1 and 𝛃mn={g/(1+g)}β^ (Proposition 7.6.3(c)). Substitute in Theorem 12.6.2(a). As g→∞ the centre and the matrix tend to those of least squares, but ν=2a0+n stays larger than n−p and τ2=bn/an tends to (2b0+SSE)/(2a0+n), not to s2. The interval does not become the t interval: that needs the flat prior on σ2 as well, with a0=−p/2 and b0=0 (Corollary 7.6.4).

Exercise (B2)

Two future observations are taken at the same x0. Show that, under the posterior predictive distribution, they are correlated, and find the correlation. Why are they correlated when their errors are independent? Are they independent under the frequentist model?

Solution. By Theorem 12.6.2(c) with X0 having two equal rows x0⊤, the scale matrix is τ2(I2+h11⊤) with h=x0⊤Vnx0, so the correlation is h/(1+h). Both observations share the unknown 𝛃β (and σ2), and uncertainty about the shared mean correlates them. Under the frequentist model with fixed parameters the two observations are independent, but the two prediction errors Y0j−Y^0 share Y^0 and have the same correlation h0/(1+h0) under the flat prior.

C. Going deeper

Exercise (C1)

Let X have rank r<p and take the flat prior 𝛃π(β,σ2)∝1/σ2. Show that the posterior of 𝛃β is improper, but that for estimable 𝛌𝛃λ⊤β the posterior of 𝛌𝛃(λ⊤β,σ2) can be defined by reparameterizing with 𝛄𝛃γ=U⊤β and 𝛅𝛃δ=N⊤β, where the columns of U and N are orthonormal bases of C(X⊤) and N(X), and integrating over nothing but 𝛄γ. Show that the resulting credible interval is the t interval of Theorem 12.1.1 with n−r degrees of freedom, provided the flat prior is taken as 1/σ2 in 𝛄(γ,σ2) alone.

Solution. The likelihood depends on 𝛃β only through 𝛃𝛄Xβ=XUγ, since XN=0. So the posterior is flat in 𝛅δ, which is not integrable: the posterior of 𝛃β is improper. In the model 𝛄Y∼Nn(XUγ,σ2I) the matrix XU has full column rank r, and Corollary 7.6.4 with p replaced by r gives a proper posterior with t(n−r) marginals. An estimable 𝛌λ lies in C(X⊤)=C(U), so 𝛌𝛌λ=UU⊤λ and 𝛌𝛃𝛌𝛄λ⊤β=(U⊤λ)⊤γ involves 𝛄γ only. Its credible interval is centred at 𝛌𝛄𝛌𝛃(U⊤λ)⊤γ^=λ⊤β^ with scale 𝛌𝛌s2(U⊤λ)⊤(U⊤X⊤XU)−1(U⊤λ), and U(U⊤X⊤XU)−1U⊤ is a generalized inverse of X⊤X (check X⊤XU(U⊤X⊤XU)−1U⊤X⊤X=X⊤X using X=XUU⊤), so the scale is 𝛌𝛌s2λ⊤Gλ.
