28.2  Restricted eigenvalue conditions

In Parts II and III, the precision of least squares was governed by the smallest eigenvalue of X⊤X: the worst variance of 𝛃a⊤β^ over unit vectors a is σ2/λmin(X⊤X) (Proposition 19.6.1). When p>n that eigenvalue is zero. This section explains which weaker condition takes its place once 𝛃β is assumed sparse, and shows with numbers how demanding it is.

28.2.1. Notation

Throughout Sections 28.2–28.5 the columns of X are scaled so that ‖xj‖2=n for every j, and Σ^=X⊤X/n is the sample Gram matrix, with unit diagonal. For a vector v∈Rp and a set S⊆{1,…,p}, vS is the vector that agrees with v on S and is zero off S, so that v=vS+vSc. The ℓ1 and ℓ∞ norms are ‖v‖1=∑j|vj| and ‖v‖∞=maxj|vj|, and ‖v‖ is still the Euclidean norm. The support of 𝛃β is S={j:βj≠0}, and s=|S|. A vector with at most s nonzero entries is s-sparse. By Cauchy–Schwarz (Proposition 1.1.1), for every v,

‖vS‖1≤s‖vS‖≤s‖v‖.
(28.2.1)

28.2.2. Sparsity restores identifiability

If 𝛃β is only known to lie in Rp, then 𝛃β and 𝛃β+v with v∈N(X) give the same mean, and nothing can separate them. If 𝛃β is known to be sparse, most of those alternatives are excluded.

Proposition 28.2.1 (Identifiability of sparse vectors)

Distinct s-sparse vectors 𝛃𝛃β≠β′ always have 𝛃𝛃Xβ≠Xβ′ iff every set of 2s columns of X is linearly independent. In particular this requires 2s≤n.

Proof. If 𝛃𝛃β,β′ are s-sparse and 𝛃𝛃Xβ=Xβ′, then 𝛃𝛃v=β−β′ is a vector in N(X) with at most 2s nonzero entries. It is zero if every 2s columns are independent. Conversely, if some 2s columns are dependent, there is a nonzero v∈N(X) supported on them. Split its support into two sets of at most s indices, T1 and T2, and put 𝛃β=vT1, 𝛃β′=−vT2. These are distinct s-sparse vectors with 𝛃𝛃Xβ−Xβ′=Xv=0. Finally, 2s vectors in Rn can be independent only if 2s≤n.

Identifiability is a statement about exactly sparse vectors and noiseless data. For estimation we need a quantitative version that allows for noise and for estimates that are only approximately sparse. The key observation, proved in Section 28.3, is that the error of the lasso, 𝛃𝛃Δ=β^λ−β, lies with high probability in a particular cone. Its ℓ1 mass off the support is at most three times its mass on the support. Curvature of the least squares criterion is needed only in the directions of that cone.

28.2.3. Two conditions on a cone

Definition 28.2.1 (Compatibility and restricted eigenvalue conditions)

Let S⊆{1,…,p} have s≥1 elements, and let

K(S)={v∈Rp: ‖vSc‖1≤3‖vS‖1}.
(28.2.2)

The compatibility constant and the restricted eigenvalue of the design for S are ϕ2(S)=minv∈K(S),v≠0 sv⊤Σ^v‖vS‖12,κ2(S)=minv∈K(S),v≠0 v⊤Σ^v‖v‖2. The design satisfies the compatibility condition for S if ϕ2(S)>0, and the restricted eigenvalue condition for S if κ2(S)>0. The same definitions with a nonnegative definite matrix Σ in place of Σ^ give ϕΣ2(S) and κΣ2(S).

A nonzero v∈K(S) has vS≠0, since otherwise ‖vSc‖1≤0. So both ratios are defined. Both are unchanged when v is rescaled, and the sets {v∈K(S):‖v‖=1} and {v∈K(S):‖vS‖1=1} are closed and bounded. So the minima are attained, which justifies writing min. The constant 3 comes from the choice of penalty in Section 28.3. Other choices give other constants and the same theory.

The cone contains the s-dimensional subspace of vectors supported on S but no larger subspace (Exercise (C1)). So the null space of X, of dimension at least p−n, can still avoid it.

Proposition 28.2.2 (Basic facts)

  • (a) λmin(Σ^)≤κ2(S)≤ϕ2(S). If p>n, then λmin(Σ^)=0.

  • (b) κ2(S)>0 iff ϕ2(S)>0 iff N(X)∩K(S)={0}.

  • (c) (Transfer.) If Σ is symmetric and maxj,k|Σ^jk−Σjk|≤δ, then κ2(S)≥κΣ2(S)−16sδ and ϕ2(S)≥ϕΣ2(S)−16sδ.

  • (d) (Coherence.) If μ=maxj≠k|Σ^jk| is the mutual coherence of the scaled design, then κ2(S)≥1−16sμ and ϕ2(S)≥1−16sμ.

Proof.

  • (a) The first inequality holds because κ2(S) minimizes the Rayleigh quotient of Σ^ over a subset of nonzero vectors, while λmin(Σ^) minimizes it over all of them (Theorem 1.7.7). For the second, Eq. 28.2.1 gives ‖vS‖12≤s‖v‖2, so sv⊤Σ^v/‖vS‖12≥v⊤Σ^v/‖v‖2≥κ2(S) for every v∈K(S). If p>n, then rank(Σ^)≤n<p, so Σ^ is singular.

  • (b) v⊤Σ^v=‖Xv‖2/n vanishes iff Xv=0. Since the minima are attained, each constant is zero iff some nonzero v∈K(S) has Xv=0.

  • (c) For v∈K(S), ‖v‖1=‖vS‖1+‖vSc‖1≤4‖vS‖1. Hence |v⊤(Σ^−Σ)v|≤∑j,k|vj||vk|δ=δ‖v‖12≤16δ‖vS‖12≤16sδ‖v‖2, the last step by Eq. 28.2.1. Dividing by ‖v‖2 gives v⊤Σ^v/‖v‖2≥κΣ2(S)−16sδ. Dividing the middle bound by ‖vS‖12/s gives sv⊤Σ^v/‖vS‖12≥ϕΣ2(S)−16sδ.

  • (d) Take Σ=I in (c), with δ=μ (the diagonals agree). Then κI2(S)=1, and ϕI2(S)≥κI2(S)=1 by (a) applied to I.

By (b) the two conditions agree about whether the constants vanish, but by (a) their sizes, which enter the bounds, can differ a great deal: a lower bound on κ2(S) is the stronger assumption. The fast prediction rate of Section 28.3 uses ϕ2(S), the ℓ2 bound κ2(S). Part (c) carries a population condition to the sample when Σ^ is uniformly close to Σ, and (d) is the case Σ=I.

28.2.4. When the conditions hold

Populations. If λmin(Σ)>0, part (a) applied to Σ gives ϕΣ2(S)≥κΣ2(S)≥λmin(Σ). For example, the equicorrelation matrix Σ=(1−ρ)I+ρ11⊤, 0≤ρ<1, has v⊤Σv=(1−ρ)‖v‖2+ρ(1⊤v)2≥(1−ρ)‖v‖2, so both constants are at least 1−ρ for every p and S. The difficulty lies in going from Σ to Σ^ when p>n.

Random designs. Suppose the rows of X are independent Np(0,Σ) vectors with unit variances. A union bound over the p2 entries of Σ^−Σ, each an average of n products of normal variables, gives maxjk|Σ^jk−Σjk|≤Clog⁡p/n with high probability once n≥C′log⁡p. The tail bound for each entry is not the normal bound of the next section, since products of normal variables have heavier tails; it is a Bernstein-type inequality, not proved here (see Wainwright 2019, chapter 2). Part (c) then gives a positive restricted eigenvalue once n exceeds a multiple of s2log⁡p. This is wasteful, because it bounds v⊤(Σ^−Σ)v by the worst entry. Raskutti, Wainwright and Yu (2010) proved, by an argument we do not reproduce, that with probability tending to one ‖Xv‖n ≥ c1‖Σ1/2v‖−c2log⁡pn‖v‖1for all v∈Rp, for constants c1,c2>0. On the cone, ‖v‖1≤4s‖v‖, so this gives κ(S)≥c1λmin(Σ)1/2−4c2slog⁡p/n, which is positive once n exceeds a multiple of slog⁡p, possibly far smaller than p.

28.2.5. When they fail

The conditions fail when a vector of the cone is (nearly) invisible to X.

Duplicated columns. If xk=xj with j∈S and k∉S, then v=ej−ek has ‖vSc‖1=‖vS‖1=1, so it lies in the cone, and Xv=0. Both constants are zero. Nearly equal columns give nearly zero constants (Exercise (B1)). No estimator can say whether the effect belongs to xj or to its copy.

Short combinations of inactive columns. More generally, suppose an active column is a combination of inactive ones, xj=XSca with ‖a‖1≤3. Then v=ej−a (with a placed in the coordinates of Sc) lies in the cone and Xv=0. When p>n the inactive columns usually span Rn, so the conditions require every such combination to have a large ℓ1 norm.

Too many active variables for the sample size. Even with independent regressors and a population compatibility constant of one, the sample constant can be close to zero if s is large relative to n/log⁡p. A combination of hundreds of inactive columns with total ℓ1 weight 3 can then imitate XSvS closely. The next example measures this.

Example (Compatibility constants of Gaussian designs)

Take p=1000 regressors, S={1,…,s}, and three random designs, with columns scaled so that ‖xj‖2=n: independent standard normal entries with n=200 (the design used in Section 28.3) and with n=400, and equicorrelated normal regressors with ρ=0.5 and n=400. The compatibility constant is a minimum of a convex quadratic over the cone. For each sign pattern of vS the problem is convex, and a duality gap certifies the computed value. The table gives ϕ2(S) and the mutual coherence μ; its rows are labelled by the common correlation ρ.

Design μ s=1 s=2 s=5 Population
ρ=0, n=200 0.350 0.328 0.146 0.0045 1
ρ=0, n=400 0.238 0.479 0.384 0.177 1
ρ=0.5, n=400 0.641 0.266 0.192 0.089 ≥0.5

The sample constants lie far below the population values, even for s=1, and fall quickly with s. At n=200 and s=5 the constant 0.0045 makes the fast-rate bound of the next section numerically useless for that design; doubling n helps a great deal. The coherence bound of Proposition 28.2.2(d) is useless throughout: with μ=0.350, 1−16sμ is −4.60 already at s=1.

import numpy as np

def proj_simplex(u):
    """Euclidean projection onto {u >= 0, sum(u) = 1}."""
    v = np.sort(u)[::-1]
    css = np.cumsum(v) - 1
    k = np.flatnonzero(v - css / np.arange(1, len(u) + 1) > 0)[-1]
    return np.maximum(u - css[k] / (k + 1), 0)

def proj_l1(w, radius):
    """Euclidean projection onto the l1 ball of the given radius."""
    if np.abs(w).sum() <= radius:
        return w
    return np.sign(w) * proj_simplex(np.abs(w) / radius) * radius

def compatibility(X, S, radius=3.0, iters=4000):
    """Return phi^2(S) and a certified lower bound (value minus duality gap)."""
    n, p = X.shape
    s = len(S)
    Sc = np.setdiff1d(np.arange(p), S)
    A, C = X[:, S], X[:, Sc]
    L = 2 * s / n * np.linalg.norm(X, 2) ** 2         # Lipschitz constant of the gradient
    best, lower = np.inf, np.inf
    for code in range(2 ** (s - 1)):                  # sign patterns of v_S up to a global sign
        sg = np.array([1.0] + [-1.0 if (code >> k) & 1 else 1.0 for k in range(s - 1)])
        u, w = np.full(s, 1 / s), np.zeros(len(Sc))
        yu, yw, t = u.copy(), w.copy(), 1.0
        for _ in range(iters):                        # accelerated projected gradient
            r = A @ (sg * yu) + C @ yw
            gu, gw = 2 * s / n * sg * (A.T @ r), 2 * s / n * (C.T @ r)
            u_new, w_new = proj_simplex(yu - gu / L), proj_l1(yw - gw / L, radius)
            t_new = (1 + np.sqrt(1 + 4 * t * t)) / 2
            yu = u_new + (t - 1) / t_new * (u_new - u)
            yw = w_new + (t - 1) / t_new * (w_new - w)
            u, w, t = u_new, w_new, t_new
        r = A @ (sg * u) + C @ w
        value = s / n * r @ r
        gu, gw = 2 * s / n * sg * (A.T @ r), 2 * s / n * (C.T @ r)
        gap = gu @ u - gu.min() + gw @ w + radius * np.abs(gw).max()
        best, lower = min(best, value), min(lower, value - gap)
    return best, lower

rng = np.random.default_rng(2820)
n, p = 100, 300
X = rng.normal(size=(n, p))
X /= np.sqrt(np.sum(X ** 2, axis=0) / n)            # ||x_j||^2 = n
print("smallest eigenvalue of X^T X / n:", np.linalg.eigvalsh(X.T @ X / n)[0])
for s in [1, 2, 3]:
    value, lower = compatibility(X, np.arange(s), iters=1500)
    print(f"s = {s}: phi^2 = {value:.3f}")

Remark (Checking the conditions in practice). The constants depend on the unknown S and cannot be checked from data. They describe the designs for which the theory is informative: those in which no short combination of columns imitates another. With groups of nearly equivalent regressors, common in genomics, the lasso may still predict well (by the slow rate of Section 28.3), but its choice among them is arbitrary.

28.2.6. Exercises

A. Check your understanding

Exercise (A1)

Let p=2 and S={1}. Sketch K(S) and show that it is not convex. Show that for general S it is the union of 2s convex cones, one for each sign pattern of vS. This is why the example above solves one convex problem per sign pattern.

B. Practice

Exercise (B1)

Let j∈S and k∉S. Show that ϕ2(S)≤s‖xj−xk‖2/n and κ2(S)≤‖xj−xk‖2/(2n). If xj⊤xk/n=r (the cosine of the angle between the two columns, which is their sample correlation when the columns are centred), conclude that ϕ2(S)≤2s(1−r).

Solution. Take v=ej−ek∈K(S). Then ‖vS‖1=1, ‖v‖2=2 and v⊤Σ^v=‖xj−xk‖2/n, which gives both bounds. With ‖xj‖2=‖xk‖2=n, ‖xj−xk‖2/n=2−2xj⊤xk/n=2(1−r).

Exercise (B2)

For Σ=(1−ρ)I+ρ11⊤ with 0≤ρ<1 and p≥2, show that κΣ2(S)=1−ρ for every S. Is the equicorrelation structure a hard case for the restricted eigenvalue condition?

Solution. The lower bound 1−ρ was shown in the text. For equality, it is enough to find a nonzero v∈K(S) with 1⊤v=0, since then v⊤Σv=(1−ρ)‖v‖2. If s≥2, take ej−ek with j,k∈S. If s=1, take ej−ek with S={j} and any k≠j; it lies in the cone because 1≤3. The population condition is therefore as good as the smallest eigenvalue, and equicorrelation is not a hard case in the population. It is harder in the sample (the table above), and harder still for exact support recovery, as Section 28.4 shows for a related design.

C. Going deeper

Exercise (C1)

Show that every linear subspace contained in K(S) has dimension at most s, and that the bound is attained. Deduce that if dim⁡N(X)>p−s then the conditions fail for every S of size s, whereas dim⁡N(X)=p−n with n≥s is not excluded by this dimension count.

Exercise (C2)

(Exact recovery by ℓ1 minimization.) Say X has the null space property for S if every nonzero v∈N(X) has ‖vSc‖1>‖vS‖1. Show that this holds iff, for every 𝛃β supported on S, 𝛃β is the unique solution of: minimize ‖b‖1 subject to 𝛃Xb=Xβ. Relate the property to Proposition 28.2.2(b) with the constant 3 replaced by 1.

Solution. Suppose the property holds, 𝛃β is supported on S, and 𝛃Xb=Xβ with 𝛃b≠β. Then 𝛃v=b−β∈N(X) is nonzero, and 𝛃bS=β+vS, bSc=vSc, so 𝛃𝛃‖b‖1≥‖β‖1−‖vS‖1+‖vSc‖1>‖β‖1. So 𝛃β is the unique minimizer. Conversely, if some nonzero v∈N(X) has ‖vSc‖1≤‖vS‖1, put 𝛃β=vS and b=−vSc. Then 𝛃𝛃Xb=Xβ−Xv=Xβ, 𝛃b≠β and 𝛃‖b‖1≤‖β‖1, so 𝛃β is not the unique minimizer. The property says exactly that N(X) meets the cone {‖vSc‖1≤‖vS‖1} only at zero. This is part (b) with the constant 1 in place of 3. The noisy problem needs the larger cone because the penalty must also absorb the noise.
