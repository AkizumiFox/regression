Exponential Families and the Generalized Linear Model

Every chapter so far has modelled a response whose scatter about a linear mean is, if not normal, at least additive and of constant shape. Counts, proportions, waiting times and insurance claims are none of these: their means are confined to an interval, their spread grows with their mean, their distributions are skewed by construction. This chapter builds the framework that handles them — a response from an exponential dispersion family, a linear predictor 𝛃x(i)⊤β, and a link joining the two. Least squares gives way to maximum likelihood, the normal equations to the likelihood equations, and the closed-form estimate to an iteration that turns out to be weighted least squares, run until it stops changing. Exact distribution theory gives way to asymptotics, so every claim here comes with conditions attached.

What you need. Chapter 5 and Chapter 6 (the linear model, projection, leverage), Section 7.3 and Section 7.4 (maximum likelihood Theorem 7.3.1, the score and information Proposition 7.4.7, exponential families Lemma 7.4.2), Chapter 11 (the likelihood ratio test Theorem 11.4.1 and the trinity Proposition 11.4.2), Section 21.3 (weighted least squares Theorem 21.3.1). Chapter 20 (residuals and influence, and iteratively reweighted least squares Proposition 20.6.1) and Chapter 29 (AIC) are used in the last section.
