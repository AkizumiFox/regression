35.2  Probit and complementary log–log links

Nothing forces a binary model to use the logit. Any function mapping the real line onto (0,1) can serve as the response function π=h(η), and its inverse is then the link. The candidates that survive in practice come with a story about how the binary outcome was produced, because such a story says what the coefficients mean. There are two such stories, and they give the probit and the complementary log–log link.

35.2.1. Thresholds

Definition 35.2.1 (Threshold model)

Let ε1,…,εn be independent with common continuous distribution function F, and let 𝛃Yi={1,εi≤x(i)⊤β,0,otherwise. The variable 𝛃x(i)⊤β−εi is the latent response, and Yi records the sign of it.

Immediately 𝛃𝛃πi=Pr(εi≤x(i)⊤β)=F(x(i)⊤β), so a threshold model is a binary regression with response function F and link F−1, and the content lies in which F the situation suggests.

Proposition 35.2.1 (Three links and where they come from)

In Definition 35.2.1:

  • (a) if F is the standard logistic distribution function F(u)=eu/(1+eu), the model is Eq. 35.1.1; if F=Φ, it is the probit model 𝛃Φ−1(πi)=x(i)⊤β. Both F are symmetric about zero, so both links satisfy g(π)=−g(1−π);

  • (b) if F(u)=1−exp⁡(−eu), the distribution function of the smallest extreme value law, the model is the complementary log–log model 𝛃log⁡{−log⁡(1−πi)}=x(i)⊤β. This F is not symmetric: π leaves 0 slowly and approaches 1 abruptly. Reflecting it, that is, applying the complementary log–log link to 1−π and changing the sign of 𝛃β, gives the log–log model 𝛃−log⁡{−log⁡πi}=x(i)⊤β, with the two tails exchanged;

  • (c) suppose a positive lifetime T has hazard function 𝛄λ(t∣x)=λ0(t)exp⁡(x⊤γ), and that Y records whether the event has occurred by a fixed time t0. Then Y follows a complementary log–log model with linear predictor 𝛄log⁡Λ0(t0)+x⊤γ, where Λ0 is the integrated baseline hazard. Moreover, if g is a continuous strictly increasing link with the property that for every c>0 replacing the hazard λ by cλ changes g(π) by a quantity κ(c) depending on c alone, and not on Λ0(t0) or x, then g is an increasing affine function of the complementary log–log link.

Proof.

  • (a) and (b) are the identity π=F(η) read backwards, together with the computation F−1(π)=log⁡{π/(1−π)}, Φ−1(π) and log⁡{−log⁡(1−π)} for the three F. For the reflection in (b), if 1−π=1−exp⁡(−e−η) then log⁡{−log⁡π}=−η.

  • (b) The survivor function of a hazard model is 𝛄Pr(T>t0∣x)=exp⁡{−∫0t0λ(u∣x)du}=exp⁡{−Λ0(t0)ex⊤γ}. Hence 𝛄1−π=exp⁡(−elog⁡Λ0(t0)+x⊤γ), and taking log⁡(−log⋅) of both sides gives the claim. For uniqueness, write 𝛄H=Λ0(t0)ex⊤γ for the cumulative hazard and G(u)=g(1−e−eu) with u=log⁡H, so that u ranges over the whole line as c and the baseline vary. The hypothesis reads G(u+log⁡c)=G(u)+κ(c) for all real u and all c>0, so that κ(v)=G(u+v)−G(u) does not depend on u, and hence κ(v+v′)=κ(v)+κ(v′) for all real v,v′. Continuity of g makes κ continuous, and the only continuous solutions of that additive Cauchy equation are the linear ones, so κ(v)=av; monotonicity gives a>0. Then G(u)=G(0)+au. Undoing the substitution, g(π)=alog⁡{−log⁡(1−π)}+b.

Part (c) is why the complementary log–log link is standard for binary data derived from times: a “yes” by a fixed follow-up date, a machine failed within a warranty period. Its coefficients are log hazard ratios, whereas logistic coefficients for the same data would be log odds ratios depending on t0 (Exercise (B2)).

35.2.2. What the latent scale can and cannot tell you

Proposition 35.2.2 (Scale is not identified)

In Definition 35.2.1, replace εi by σεi+τ with σ>0, and 𝛃β by 𝛃σβ+τe1, where e1 picks out an intercept column. The observed responses have exactly the same distribution. Consequently 𝛃β is identified only up to the location and scale of the latent errors, while ratios βj/βk of coefficients of non-intercept covariates are identified.

Proof. The event 𝛃{σεi+τ≤x(i)⊤(σβ+τe1)} is 𝛃{εi≤x(i)⊤β}, because the intercept column is identically 1 so x(i)⊤e1=1 and the τ cancels, after which dividing by σ removes it. The two models therefore give the same πi for every i. Ratios of non-intercept coefficients are unchanged by the substitution.

This is why the probit fixes Var(ε)=1 and the logistic model fixes Var(ε)=π2/3: a convention must be adopted. It is also why probit and logit coefficients are not comparable until one is rescaled. Matching the standard deviations multiplies a probit coefficient by π/3=1.8138; matching the slope at π=1/2 instead, where the logistic density is 1/4 and the normal density is ϕ(0), multiplies it by 4ϕ(0)=1.5958. The conventions differ by about fourteen per cent, a fair measure of how meaningless the comparison is in the tails.

Warning. The latent-variable story tempts one into calling the probit coefficients “the real effects, on the underlying continuous variable”. They are not: the latent variable is not observed and its scale is a convention that Proposition 35.2.2 says the data cannot pin down. What the story buys is a rationale for a particular F.

35.2.3. How much does the choice matter?

Near π=1/2 all reasonable links agree, because smooth response curves look alike near their steepest point. They separate in the tails: the logistic tails are exponential, the normal tails decay like e−η2/2, and the complementary log–log is not symmetric at all.

Example (The survey under three links)

Fitting the model of Example (Voting intention in the 1996 American election) with the three links gives the following coefficients of party identification.

link β^ rescaled by 1.8138 maximized log-likelihood
logit 1.219 — −264.14
probit 0.673 1.221 −265.96
complementary log–log 0.801 — −273.48

The rescaled probit coefficient reproduces the logistic one to two decimals, and the largest difference between the two sets of fitted probabilities is 0.0305. The complementary log–log fit is a different animal: its fitted probabilities differ from the logistic ones by as much as 0.1596, and with equal numbers of parameters AIC prefers the logit by 18.67. Here the choice between symmetric links is immaterial and the choice of an asymmetric one is not (Figure 35.2.1).

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import statsmodels.api as sm

anes = sm.datasets.anes96.load_pandas().data
X = pd.DataFrame({"const": 1.0, "PID": anes["PID"], "age": anes["age"] / 10,
                  "educ": anes["educ"], "income": anes["income"]})
y = anes["vote"]

links = {"logit": sm.families.links.Logit(), "probit": sm.families.links.Probit(),
         "cloglog": sm.families.links.CLogLog()}
fits = {k: sm.GLM(y, X, family=sm.families.Binomial(link=v)).fit() for k, v in links.items()}

table = pd.DataFrame({k: f.params for k, f in fits.items()})
table["probit x 1.814"] = table["probit"] * np.pi / np.sqrt(3)
print(table.round(3).to_string())
print("maximized log-likelihoods:",
      {k: round(f.llf, 2) for k, f in fits.items()})

The choice therefore matters when the interesting probabilities are extreme, where the links disagree and few observations settle the disagreement; when a mechanism names the link, as in Proposition 35.2.1(c); and under the retrospective sampling of Section 35.5, where the logit is the only link whose coefficients survive.

35.2.4. Exercises

A. Check your understanding

Exercise (A1)

Show that the logit and probit links satisfy g(π)=−g(1−π) while the complementary log–log link does not. Deduce that for the first two, exchanging the labels 0 and 1 merely reverses the sign of every coefficient, and say what the exchange does under the complementary log–log link.

Solution. For the logit, log⁡{(1−π)/π}=−log⁡{π/(1−π)}; for the probit, Φ−1(1−π)=−Φ−1(π). Relabelling therefore turns η into −η, so 𝛃β becomes 𝛃−β and the fit is the same model. For the complementary log–log link, relabelling produces the log–log model of Proposition 35.2.1(b): a genuinely different fit.

B. Practice

Exercise (B1)

Verify that F(u)=1−exp⁡(−eu) is a distribution function, and that if ε has this law then Eε=−γ and Varε=π2/6, where γ=0.5772 is Euler’s constant. Deduce the factor that makes a complementary log–log coefficient’s latent scale comparable with a logistic one.

Solution. F is continuous and increasing with limits 0 and 1. Substituting v=eu turns −ε into a standard Gumbel variable, whose mean is γ and whose variance is π2/6; reflecting gives the stated moments. The standard deviation is π/6=1.2825 against π/3=1.8138 for the logistic, so the multiplier is 2. The location differs too, which shifts the intercept, so only the slopes are comparable.

Exercise (B2)

Follow-up is divided into intervals (tk−1,tk]. Let Yik=1 if subject i has the event in interval k, given that it has not had it before. Show that a proportional hazards model for the underlying continuous time implies 𝛄log⁡{−log⁡(1−Pr(Yik=1))}=αk+x(i)⊤γ with αk=log⁡{Λ0(tk)−Λ0(tk−1)}, and explain how to fit it with ordinary binary regression software.

C. Going deeper

Exercise (C1)

Consider the asymmetric family of Aranda-Ordaz (1981), π=1−(1+αeη)−1/α for α>0. Show that α=1 gives the logistic response function and α→0 the complementary log–log one. Explain how to test α=1 with grouped data, and why the test has little power with ungrouped data.
