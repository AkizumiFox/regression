Bootstrap and Permutation Inference

Every test and interval in Part III took its reference distribution from the normal model. This chapter asks whether the reference distribution can be taken from the data instead. The bootstrap imitates the process that produced the data by resampling residuals, whole cases, or residuals with random signs, and each choice assumes a different model. A permutation test rearranges the data in ways that leave their distribution unchanged under the null hypothesis, and is exact whenever such rearrangements exist. In regression they exist only for simple hypotheses, and the Freedman–Lane scheme shows how to get close in other cases.
