Quadratic Forms and Their Distributions

Every sum of squares in a linear model is a quadratic form Y⊤AY in a normal vector, and every F and t statistic is a ratio built from such forms. This chapter works out their laws: the noncentral chi-squared, F and t distributions and the monotonicity of their tails; the mean, variance and a canonical representation of an arbitrary quadratic form; exactly when a quadratic form is chi-squared; when two forms, or a form and a linear function, are independent; and Cochran’s theorem, which says when a sum of squares splits into independent chi-squared pieces.

What you need. Chapter 1 (spectral theorem, symmetric idempotent matrices, trace), Chapter 2 (means and covariances of random vectors) and Chapter 3 (the multivariate normal distribution and its independence properties). Orthogonal projections appear here only as symmetric idempotent matrices. Chapter 6 develops their geometry.
