Counts

A count has no upper bound, cannot be negative, and is nearly always more variable when it is large than when it is small. The linear model fits none of these descriptions and no transformation repairs all of them at once. The generalized linear model does better: put the linear predictor on the log scale, so that the mean is multiplicative, and let the distribution supply a variance that grows with the mean. That is the Poisson log-linear model, the starting point of this chapter.

It is only a starting point. Real counts are almost always more variable than the Poisson allows, often have far more zeros, and are sometimes not a sample of independent events at all but a contingency table in disguise. Each complaint has a model attached to it: exposure and rates, the exact connection between Poisson counts and multinomial proportions, the negative binomial, and the two standard ways to model an excess of zeros.
