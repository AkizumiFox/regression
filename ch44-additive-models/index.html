Additive, Geoadditive and Structured Additive Models

Chapter 43 learned to estimate one unknown curve. Real data sets have a dozen covariates, and one unknown surface in a dozen dimensions is hopeless: no neighbourhood of a point holds enough observations to average. The way out is a structural assumption weaker than linearity but far stronger than nothing — the covariates act additively, each through its own unknown function — which turns a hopeless problem into a stack of one-dimensional ones held together by a single penalized criterion.

This chapter builds that framework and keeps widening it. A coefficient may vary smoothly with a second covariate. A term may be a spatial effect, penalized towards its neighbours on a map or towards a smooth surface over coordinates. A term may be a random intercept, a penalized term whose penalty is the identity. All of these are one model — a structured additive predictor — with one estimation theory: a quadratic penalty per term, effective degrees of freedom from a trace, smoothing parameters from a restricted likelihood, and a Bayesian reading in which every penalty is a prior. The last section puts a link and a response distribution in front of that predictor and closes the circle with Part VIII.

What you need. Chapter 6 (projections, the trace as a rank, Proposition 6.3.4, and Theorem 6.6.2), Chapter 26 (collinearity and the variance inflation factor, Definition 26.2.1), Chapter 27 and Chapter 30 (ridge, penalized least squares and effective degrees of freedom, Theorem 27.2.2 and Definition 30.1.1), Chapter 29 (cross-validation and information criteria, Theorem 29.3.2, Definition 29.3.2, Proposition 29.5.1), Chapter 32 (mixed models, BLUP and REML, Definition 32.2.1, Theorem 32.3.1, Theorem 32.4.2), Chapter 34 (the generalized linear model and IRLS, Definition 34.2.1, Theorem 34.4.1), Chapter 38 (quasi-likelihood and dispersion, Definition 38.2.1, Proposition 38.3.2), Chapter 39 (the Bayesian machinery, Definition 39.4.1, Proposition 39.5.1) and, above all, Chapter 43, whose P-spline blocks and smoothing-parameter criteria are used here as given.

Remark (Notation in this chapter). A term is a pair: a design block Zj of size n×dj and a nonnegative definite penalty Pj, with coefficients 𝛄γj and smoothing parameter λj — the P, 𝛄γ and λ of Chapter 43, now carrying a term index. So Z keeps its meaning from Part VII, the design of the coefficients that are shrunk, and Section 44.5 shows the two uses are the same. Unpenalized columns stay in X with coefficients 𝛃β; A=Z⊤WZ+P is the penalized cross-product, W the working weights of Chapter 34, S the smoother matrix and Cj the operator producing the jth component.
