44.4  Spatial effects

Many regression data sets carry a location: the district a flat is in, the county a case was reported from, the coordinates of a soil sample. Location is rarely of interest in itself; it stands in for everything unmeasured that varies smoothly over space — climate, soil, infrastructure, local labour markets — and leaving it out puts all of that into the error and biases the coefficient of any spatially patterned covariate. Putting it in needs a design block and a penalty, like any smooth covariate. The only question is what “smooth over space” should mean, and there are two answers, one per kind of location data.

44.4.1. The geoadditive model

Definition 44.4.1 (Geoadditive model)

Let si be the location of observation i. The geoadditive model is the additive model Eq. 44.1.1 with one extra term,

𝛃yi=x(i)⊤β+∑j=1qfj(zij)+fgeo(si)+εi,∑ifgeo(si)=0.
(44.4.1)

Two cases are distinguished by the kind of location.

  • (a) (Areal data.) si∈{1,…,D} names one of D regions. The design is the incidence matrix Zgeo with (Zgeo)is=1 if si=s and 0 otherwise, the coefficient γs is the effect of region s, and the penalty is built from a neighbourhood structure: a symmetric relation s∼t saying which regions share a border.

  • (b) (Point-referenced data.) si∈R2 is a coordinate pair. The design is (Zgeo)il=r(‖si−κl‖) for a set of knots κ1,…,κL and a radial function r, and the penalty is Pgeo={r(‖κl−κm‖)}lm.

In (a) the map is a graph and smoothness means that neighbouring regions do not differ by much; in (b) the map is a plane and smoothness means what it means for a surface. Both are pairs (Zgeo,Pgeo), so Sections 44.1 and 44.2 apply without a word of change.

44.4.2. Markov random fields on a map

For areal data, write ns for the number of neighbours of region s and define the neighbourhood penalty

𝛄𝛄γ⊤Pγ=∑s∼t(γs−γt)2,Kst={ns,t=s,−1,t∼s,0,otherwise,
(44.4.2)

where the sum runs over unordered neighbour pairs: the first-difference penalty of Definition 43.3.1 with “next knot” replaced by “neighbouring region”, the two coinciding exactly on a chain 1∼2∼⋯∼D.

Example (Four regions in a row)

Let four districts lie in a row, 1∼2∼3∼4: P=(1−100−12−100−12−100−11), with 𝛄𝛄γ⊤Pγ=(γ1−γ2)2+(γ2−γ3)2+(γ3−γ4)2, rank 3 and null space span{1}. If district 4 is instead an island with no neighbours, its row and column vanish, the rank drops to 2 and the null space is spanned by (1,1,1,0)⊤ and (0,0,0,1)⊤: the island is not penalized at all and is estimated from its own data alone.

Proposition 44.4.1 (The neighbourhood penalty)

Let the regions 1,…,D carry a symmetric neighbourhood relation with no region its own neighbour, and let P be as in Eq. 44.4.2.

  • (a) P is symmetric and nonnegative definite, and 𝛄𝛄γ⊤Pγ=∑s∼t(γs−γt)2.

  • (b) N(P) is spanned by the indicator vectors of the connected components of the neighbourhood graph. In particular rank(P)=D−c where c is the number of components, and N(P)=span{1} iff the graph is connected.

  • (c) Treating 𝛄𝛄exp⁡{−γ⊤Pγ/(2τ2)} as a density in 𝛄γ on N(P)⊥, the conditional distribution of γs given the others, for a region with ns≥1, is normal with mean ns−1∑t∼sγt, the average of its neighbours, and variance τ2/ns. The prior is therefore a Gaussian Markov random field: each region is shrunk towards its neighbours, and a region with many neighbours is shrunk harder. A region with ns=0 contributes nothing to the quadratic form: its coefficient is unpenalized, its conditional is flat, and it is estimated from its own data alone (Example (Four regions in a row), Exercise (A1)).

Proof.

  • (a) Symmetry is immediate. Each unordered neighbour pair is counted twice in the double sum over ordered pairs, so ∑s∼t(γs−γt)2=12∑s∑t∼s(γs2−2γsγt+γt2). The first inner sum gives ∑snsγs2, and so does the third, because ∑s∑t∼sγt2=∑tntγt2 by symmetry of the relation. Hence the right-hand side is 𝛄𝛄∑snsγs2−∑s∑t∼sγsγt=γ⊤Pγ. Nonnegativity follows because the left-hand side is a sum of squares.

  • (b) By (a), 𝛄𝛄γ⊤Pγ=0 iff γs=γt across every edge, iff γ is constant on each connected component. Since P is nonnegative definite, that is equivalent to 𝛄Pγ=0 by Theorem 1.7.4, so the null space is the span of the component indicators, which are independent because the components are disjoint.

  • (c) If ns=0 then row s of P is zero and γs does not appear in the quadratic form, which is the last sentence. For ns≥1, collect the terms of 𝛄𝛄γ⊤Pγ that involve γs: nsγs2−2γs∑t∼sγt+const=ns(γs−1ns∑t∼sγt)2+const′. Dividing by 2τ2 and exponentiating gives the normal density with the stated mean and variance τ2/ns.

Part (c) is the reason for the name. The penalty does not shrink a region towards zero — that would be the ridge penalty P=I — but towards its neighbourhood, so a district with few observations borrows strength from those around it while the overall level of the map stays free. That level is the one dimension the centring constraint of Proposition 44.1.1(c) removes on a connected map; it belongs to the intercept.

Remark (Variants). Three variants fit the same framework. Weighting each pair by wst (border length, inverse centroid distance) replaces ns by ∑t∼swst and −1 by −wst. Adding ϵI makes the penalty positive definite and the prior proper, at the price of shrinking the map towards zero as well as towards itself. And a second, unstructured term with P=I on the same regions gives the convolution model of Besag, York and Mollié (1991), separating smooth spatial variation from region-specific noise.

44.4.3. Kriging bases for coordinates

For points rather than regions, smoothness comes from the theory of stationary random fields, which is to say from Part VII. Let the spatial effect be a realization of a mean-zero process {U(s)} with Cov{U(s),U(t)}=τ2r(‖s−t‖): a covariance model in the sense of Definition 33.4.1, on a continuous index set.

Proposition 44.4.2 (Low-rank kriging is a penalized term)

Let R={r(‖κl−κm‖)}lm be the correlation matrix of the field at L knots, assumed positive definite, and let u={U(κ1),…,U(κL)}⊤∼NL(0,τ2R).

  • (a) The best linear predictor of U(s) from u is U^(s)=r(s)⊤R−1u with r(s)={r(‖s−κ1‖),…,r(‖s−κL‖)}⊤. Writing 𝛄γ=R−1u, the fitted spatial effect at the observations is 𝛄Zgeoγ with (Zgeo)il=r(‖si−κl‖), and 𝛄γ∼NL(0,τ2R−1).

  • (b) Consequently the log-density of 𝛄γ contributes 𝛄𝛄γ⊤Rγ/(2τ2), so the penalized criterion Eq. 44.1.4 with Pgeo=R and λgeo=σ2/τ2 has as its minimizer the posterior mode of 𝛄γ — the kriging predictor restricted to the span of the L knots.

  • (c) Taking every distinct location as a knot recovers exact kriging, that is, generalized least squares with the covariance τ2R+σ2I of Chapter 31; taking L≪n knots is the low-rank approximation that makes the term cheap.

Proof.

  • (a) By Theorem 14.2.8 (or Theorem 32.3.1 with X absent) the best linear predictor is Cov{U(s),u}Cov(u)−1u=τ2r(s)⊤(τ2R)−1u, the stated formula; evaluating at s=si stacks into ZgeoR−1u, and the distribution of 𝛄γ=R−1u follows from Theorem 2.2.1.
  • (b) −log density of NL(0,τ2R−1) is 𝛄𝛄γ⊤Rγ/(2τ2) up to a constant. Adding it to ‖y−…‖2/(2σ2) and multiplying by 2σ2 gives Eq. 44.1.4 with the stated λ.
  • (c) With L=n distinct locations, Zgeo=R and the term contributes 𝛄Rγ=u with u penalized by u⊤R−1u; minimizing over u is the mixed model of Definition 32.2.1 with Z=I, G=τ2R and R=σ2I, whose marginal covariance is τ2R+σ2I (Eq. 32.2.2).

Idea. A spatial term is a random effect with a structured covariance — not an analogy: by Proposition 44.4.2 the penalty is the inverse prior covariance, and Section 44.5 makes that general. The Markov random field Eq. 44.4.2 says the same with a sparse precision matrix in place of a dense covariance, which is why it is cheap on large maps.

If R and Zgeo are to be sparse, the correlation function must have compact support or decay fast. The example below uses the Wendland function r(h)=(1−h/ρ)+4(1+4h/ρ), positive definite in two dimensions and zero beyond the range ρ.

44.4.4. A map

Example (Smoothing a district map)

A simulated country is a 12×12 lattice of 144 districts with between 1 and 8 observations each, 604 in all. The response has mean 2+0.7x+surface(s), with x an ordinary covariate and the surface a smooth function of the district centroid that the model does not know; the errors are normal with standard deviation 0.9.

Figure 44.4.1 compares three fits. Unpenalized district effects use 143.0 degrees of freedom and give the noisy map of panel (b), with root mean squared error 0.579 against the true surface. The neighbourhood penalty Eq. 44.4.2, with λ from the restricted likelihood, uses 45.5 and brings that down to 0.170. A kriging term on 21 knots uses 17.7 and gives 0.228: smoother still, and slightly too smooth, because the true surface has features the coarse knot grid cannot represent. The coefficient of x comes out at 0.581 with a standard error of 0.042 — against a true 0.7, about 2.8 standard errors low on this draw — and the residual standard deviation at 0.953.

The map and its penalty are built from the neighbourhood list alone:

import numpy as np

def lattice(side):
    """Rook adjacency on a side x side grid of districts, and the district centroids."""
    coords = np.array([[c + 0.5, r + 0.5] for r in range(side) for c in range(side)])
    neighbours = []
    for s in range(side * side):
        r, c = divmod(s, side)
        nb = [(r + dr) * side + (c + dc) for dr, dc in ((-1, 0), (1, 0), (0, -1), (0, 1))
              if 0 <= r + dr < side and 0 <= c + dc < side]
        neighbours.append(sorted(nb))
    return coords, neighbours

def neighbourhood_penalty(neighbours):
    """K = D - W: the number of neighbours on the diagonal, -1 for each neighbour pair."""
    d = len(neighbours)
    K = np.zeros((d, d))
    for s, nbrs in enumerate(neighbours):
        K[s, s] = len(nbrs)
        for t in nbrs:
            K[s, t] = -1.0
    return K

Warning (Spatial confounding). A spatial term competes with every covariate that is itself spatially patterned: they are concurve in the sense of Definition 44.2.1. In Example (Smoothing a district map) the covariate x was generated independently of location, so nothing was lost. When a covariate varies smoothly over the map — income across districts, exposure across counties — the spatial term can absorb a large part of its effect, and the fitted coefficient moves, sometimes dramatically, when the term is added. Neither fit is “the” answer: without the spatial term all spatial variation is attributed to the covariate, with it the smooth part goes to location. Which is right is a question about unmeasured confounders, and the fit has no opinion; Chapter 25 has the vocabulary. Report both, and say which unmeasured causes the spatial term stands in for.

44.4.5. Exercises

A. Check your understanding

Exercise (A1)

A map has two islands, each a connected group of districts, with no neighbour pairs between them. What is rank(P), what does the penalty leave unidentified, and what does the centring constraint of Proposition 44.1.1(c) fix?

B. Practice

Exercise (B1)

Show that on a chain 1∼2∼⋯∼D, the penalty Eq. 44.4.2 equals the first-difference penalty 𝛄𝛄γ⊤D1⊤D1γ of Definition 43.3.1, and that the second-difference penalty is not of the form Eq. 44.4.2 for any neighbourhood structure with nonnegative off-diagonal weights.

Exercise (B2)

Using Proposition 44.4.1(c), show that if district s has ns neighbours and its own ms observations with mean y¯s, and all other effects are held fixed, the penalized criterion is minimized over γs at a weighted average of y¯s and the neighbour mean, with weights ms and λns. What happens when ms=0?

Solution. Up to constants the terms of Eq. 44.1.4 involving γs are ms(y¯s−γs)2+λns(γs−γ¯(s))2 with γ¯(s)=ns−1∑t∼sγt, so γ^s=(msy¯s+λnsγ¯(s))/(ms+λns). With ms=0 the district is set to its neighbour average: the map interpolates over the gap.

C. Going deeper

Exercise (C1)

Let v be the vector of a covariate that is constant within each district, so that v∈C(Zgeo) for the incidence design. Show that in the unpenalized geoadditive model the coefficient of v is not identified at all, and that with a penalty it is identified but its estimate depends on λgeo, tending to the no-spatial-term estimate as λgeo→∞. What does this say about reporting such a coefficient?
