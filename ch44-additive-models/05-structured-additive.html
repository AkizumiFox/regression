44.5  Structured additive regression

Four kinds of term have appeared: a smooth function of a covariate, a coefficient varying with a modifier, an effect attached to a region of a map, an effect attached to a cluster. Each was a pair (Zj,Pj) — a design block and a nonnegative definite penalty — estimated by the same penalized criterion. This section says that out loud, shows that the framework contains the mixed models of Chapter 32 exactly, and takes from them the thing still missing: a principled way to choose the smoothing parameters.

44.5.1. One predictor

Definition 44.5.1 (Structured additive regression)

A structured additive predictor is

𝛈𝛃𝛄η=Xβ+∑j=1JZjγj,
(44.5.1)

where X collects the unpenalized columns and each term j is a pair (Zj,Pj) with Zj an n×dj design block and Pj a symmetric nonnegative definite dj×dj penalty matrix, identified by the constraint 𝛄1⊤Zjγj=0 of Proposition 44.1.1(c). A structured additive regression model (STAR) fits Eq. 44.5.1 by minimizing the penalized criterion Eq. 44.1.4, with one smoothing parameter λj≥0 per term.

The content of the definition is the catalogue of pairs. Everything this chapter and Chapter 43 have built is in Table 44.5.1, and the list is open: a new kind of effect enters as soon as somebody writes down its design and its penalty.

Table 44.5.1. The catalogue of terms. Proposition 44.5.1 (When a STAR model is identified)

In Definition 44.5.1, assume every Zj has full column rank. The penalized criterion has a unique minimizer for every λj>0 if and only if the only vector 𝛄𝛃𝛄γ=(β⊤,γ1⊤,…)⊤ with 𝛃𝛄Xβ+∑jZjγj=0 and 𝛄Pjγj=0 for every j is 𝛄γ=0. Equivalently: there is no exact concurvity among C(X) and the unpenalized parts ZjN(Pj) of the terms.

Proof. The criterion is the convex quadratic 𝛄𝛄𝛄‖y−Zγ‖2+γ⊤Pγ with P the block penalty Eq. 44.1.5, and its stationarity condition is Eq. 44.1.6 with A=Z⊤Z+P. If A is positive definite the criterion is strictly convex and Theorem 30.1.1 gives a unique minimizer. Conversely, if A is singular, pick 𝛄γ0≠0 with 𝛄Aγ0=0; expanding the criterion at a minimizer 𝛄γ^ gives 𝛄𝛄𝛄𝛄𝛄𝛄Q(γ^+tγ0)=Q(γ^)+t2γ0⊤Aγ0=Q(γ^) for every t, so the minimizer is not unique. Uniqueness for every λj>0 is therefore positive definiteness of A, that is 𝛄𝛄𝛄𝛄𝛄γ⊤Aγ=‖Zγ‖2+∑jλjγj⊤Pjγj=0 forces 𝛄γ=0. Both summands are nonnegative, so the condition is exactly the one stated, and 𝛄𝛄γj⊤Pjγj=0 is equivalent to 𝛄Pjγj=0 by Theorem 1.7.4.

Two P-spline terms can therefore be identified even when their spaces nearly intersect, because only their linear parts — the null spaces of the second-difference penalties — need be independent. That is far weaker than the requirement for the unpenalized model in Proposition 44.2.3(a), and it explains why penalized additive fits rarely fail outright: they fail softly, through inflated variances.

44.5.2. The mixed-model representation

Proposition 44.5.2 (A STAR model is a linear mixed model)

Let Pj have rank rj and spectral decomposition Pj=Qj+ΛjQj+⊤ with Λj=diag(νj1,…,νjrj) positive, and let Qj0 be an orthonormal basis of N(Pj). Put

𝛄Xj0=ZjQj0,Zj+=ZjQj+Λj−1/2,uj=Λj1/2Qj+⊤γj.
(44.5.2)

  • (a) 𝛄Zjγj=Xj0bj+Zj+uj with 𝛄bj=Qj0⊤γj, and 𝛄𝛄γj⊤Pjγj=‖uj‖2.

  • (b) With τj2=σ2/λj, the penalized criterion Eq. 44.1.4 divided by σ2 is the criterion Eq. 32.3.6 of Theorem 32.3.2 for the linear mixed model Eq. 32.2.1 with fixed design [X,X10,…,XJ0], random design [Z1+,…,ZJ+], G=diag(τ12Ir1,…,τJ2IrJ) and R=σ2I.

  • (c) Consequently the penalized estimate is the BLUP of Theorem 32.3.1; the smoothing parameters are variance ratios, λj=σ2/τj2; and the restricted likelihood Eq. 32.4.4 of the transformed model is a likelihood for (σ2,τ12,…,τJ2), hence for (λ1,…,λJ).

Proof.

  • (a) The columns of Qj+ and Qj0 form an orthonormal basis of Rdj, so 𝛄𝛄γj=Qj0bj+Qj+Qj+⊤γj and 𝛄Qj+Qj+⊤γj=Qj+Λj−1/2uj. Multiplying by Zj gives the first claim. For the second, 𝛄𝛄𝛄𝛄γj⊤Pjγj=γj⊤Qj+ΛjQj+⊤γj=‖uj‖2.
  • (b) Substituting (a) into Eq. 44.1.4 turns it into 𝛃‖y−Xβ−∑jXj0bj−∑jZj+uj‖2+∑jλj‖uj‖2, and dividing by σ2 and writing λj/σ2=1/τj2 gives R−1=σ−2I in the first term and G−1 in the second, which is Eq. 32.3.6.
  • (c) Immediate from Theorem 32.3.2(a)–(b), which identifies the minimizer of Eq. 32.3.6 with 𝛃(β^,u^), and from Definition 32.4.1 applied to the transformed model.

Idea. A penalty is a prior, and a smoothing parameter is a variance ratio. That one identification does three jobs: the null space of a penalty becomes fixed effects, which is why Table 44.5.1 records dim⁡N(Pj); it supplies a likelihood for 𝛌λ, which cross-validation does not; and it puts every result of Chapter 32 — BLUP, REML, Henderson’s equations, the posterior of Proposition 32.6.1 — at the service of smoothing.

44.5.3. Choosing the smoothing parameters

Two routes are now open. The first is prediction error: Definition 29.3.2 and the information criteria Proposition 29.2.4 apply verbatim with trS in place of the number of parameters, minimized over 𝛌λ numerically. The second is the restricted likelihood of Proposition 44.5.2(c), usually preferred: it is less prone to the occasional catastrophic undersmoothing cross-validation shows when its criterion is nearly flat, and it comes with the variance interpretation. Both choose from the data the fit uses, so Proposition 29.5.1 applies to both.

Maximizing the restricted likelihood directly is awkward, since each evaluation needs a determinant, and the updates below avoid it. What is proved is that they have the stated fixed points; that those are stationary points of the restricted likelihood is due to Schall (1991), with the general version in Wood and Fasiolo (2017).

Proposition 44.5.3 (Restricted-likelihood updates)

In the mixed-model representation of Proposition 44.5.2, the expectation-maximization algorithm for the variance components, with (b,u) as the missing data, updates

𝛄𝛄τj2,new=1rj[γ^j⊤Pjγ^j+σ2tr{(A−1)jjPj}],
(44.5.3)

where 𝛄γ^j and A are computed at the current 𝛌λ. A pair 𝛕(τ2,σ2) is a fixed point of Eq. 44.5.3 together with the update σ2,new=‖y−y^‖2/(n−df) if and only if

𝛄𝛄τ^j2=γ^j⊤Pjγ^jdfj−mj,σ^2=‖y−y^‖2n−df,λ^j=σ^2τ^j2,
(44.5.4)

with mj=dj−rj=dim⁡N(Pj) and dfj, df the effective degrees of freedom of Proposition 44.1.2.

Proof. Eq. 44.5.3 is the expectation-maximization step for a variance component in a normal linear mixed model: the complete-data estimate of τj2 is ‖uj‖2/rj, whose conditional expectation given y is ‖u^j‖2/rj+tr{Cov(uj∣y)}/rj. By Proposition 44.5.2(a), 𝛄𝛄‖u^j‖2=γ^j⊤Pjγ^j, and by Proposition 32.6.1(a) the conditional covariance of 𝛄γj is σ2(A−1)jj, so Eq. 44.5.2 gives

trCov(uj∣y)=σ2tr{Λj1/2Qj+⊤(A−1)jjQj+Λj1/2}=σ2tr{(A−1)jjPj},

the trace term as written. (The EM step itself is quoted from the mixed-model literature; what is proved here is its fixed point.)

For the fixed point, Proposition 44.1.2(b) gives dfj=dj−λjtr{(A−1)jjPj}, that is tr{(A−1)jjPj}=dj−dfjλj=(dj−dfj)τj2σ2. Substituting into Eq. 44.5.3 and demanding τj2,new=τj2, 𝛄𝛄rjτj2=γ^j⊤Pjγ^j+(dj−dfj)τj2, so 𝛄𝛄τj2(rj−dj+dfj)=γ^j⊤Pjγ^j, and rj−dj=−mj gives the first equation of Eq. 44.5.4. The statement for σ2 is its own update, and λj=σ2/τj2 is Proposition 44.5.2(c).

Read Eq. 44.5.4 as an estimate of a variance: the numerator is the roughness the term shows, the denominator dfj−mj the number of dimensions it used in showing it, discounting the unpenalized part the penalty cannot see. The solver is one assembly and one solve,

import numpy as np

def assemble(blocks, X, lambdas):
    """Stack the parametric columns and the terms into one design and one block penalty."""
    Z = np.column_stack([X] + [Zj for Zj, _ in blocks])
    K = np.zeros((Z.shape[1], Z.shape[1]))
    start = X.shape[1]
    for (_, Kj), lam in zip(blocks, lambdas):
        d = Kj.shape[0]
        K[start:start + d, start:start + d] = lam * Kj
        start += d
    return Z, K

class Fit:
    """One penalized solve: coefficients, components, operators and degrees of freedom."""

    def __init__(self, y, blocks, X, lambdas, W=None, z=None, operators=True):
        Z, K = assemble(blocks, X, lambdas)
        w = np.ones(len(y)) if W is None else W
        Zw = Z * w[:, None]
        B = Z.T @ Zw                                           # the weighted cross-product
        A = B + K
        Ainv = np.linalg.inv(A)
        self.Z, self.K, self.A, self.w = Z, K, A, w
        self.gamma = Ainv @ (Zw.T @ (y if z is None else z))
        self.fitted = Z @ self.gamma
        self.edf_total = np.trace(Ainv @ B)                    # tr(S) without forming S
        self.parts, self.edf, self.comp = [], [], []
        start = X.shape[1]
        for Zj, _ in blocks:
            sl = slice(start, start + Zj.shape[1])
            self.parts.append(Zj @ self.gamma[sl])
            self.edf.append(np.trace(Ainv[sl] @ B[:, sl]))
            start += Zj.shape[1]
        if operators:                                          # the n x n smoothers
            C = Ainv @ Zw.T
            self.S = Z @ C
            start = X.shape[1]
            for Zj, _ in blocks:
                self.comp.append(Zj @ C[start:start + Zj.shape[1]])
                start += Zj.shape[1]
        self.beta = self.gamma[:X.shape[1]]

and the updates themselves are a handful more:

def null_dim(K, tol=1e-9):
    """The dimension of the penalty null space: the part of a term that is never penalized."""
    e = np.linalg.eigvalsh(K)
    return int(np.sum(e < tol * max(e.max(), 1.0)))

def reml_lambdas(y, blocks, X, lambdas=None, iters=80, tol=1e-8):
    """Restricted-likelihood updates for a normal response: refit, then set each variance
    component to tau_j^2 = gamma_j' K_j gamma_j / (df_j - m_j) and sigma^2 to the penalized
    residual sum of squares over n - df, and put lambda_j = sigma^2 / tau_j^2."""
    lam = np.ones(len(blocks)) if lambdas is None else np.asarray(lambdas, float)
    for _ in range(iters):
        fit = Fit(y, blocks, X, lam, operators=False)
        sigma2 = np.sum((y - fit.fitted) ** 2) / (len(y) - fit.edf_total)
        new = lam.copy()
        start = X.shape[1]
        for j, (_, Kj) in enumerate(blocks):
            d = Kj.shape[0]
            g = fit.gamma[start:start + d]
            tau2 = max(g @ Kj @ g, 1e-12) / max(fit.edf[j] - null_dim(Kj), 1e-6)
            new[j] = min(max(sigma2 / tau2, 1e-8), 1e10)
            start += d
        done = np.max(np.abs(np.log(new) - np.log(lam))) < tol
        lam = new
        if done:
            break
    return lam, Fit(y, blocks, X, lam), sigma2
Example (Four kinds of term at once)

A simulated data set of 1200 observations has a binary covariate with a linear effect, a smooth effect of z1, a coefficient of u varying with z2, an areal effect on an 8×8 map of 64 districts and a random intercept for 40 clusters: four terms, 132 coefficients, smoothing parameters from Eq. 44.5.4.

The fit recovers the residual standard deviation as 0.713 (true 0.7) and the linear effect as 0.770 (true 0.8). The four terms take 7.86, 5.13, 42.92 and 36.48 effective degrees of freedom, 94.40 out of 132, with implied variance components 0.1609, 0.0170, 0.2002 and 0.2646 — the last the variance of the random intercept, truly 0.25. Root mean squared errors against the truth are 0.031, 0.045, 0.129 and 0.119, against a noise standard deviation of 0.7. Figure 44.5.1 shows all four.

The shrinkage in panels (c) and (d) is not a defect but Theorem 32.3.1: a predictor of a random effect trades bias for variance, and the slope of predicted against true falls below one by exactly the amount the estimated variance ratio dictates.

44.5.4. The Bayesian reading

Proposition 44.5.2 already contains it. Give each term the prior

𝛄𝛄𝛄π(γj∣τj2) ∝ (τj2)−rj/2exp⁡{−12τj2γj⊤Pjγj},
(44.5.5)

normal on N(Pj)⊥ and flat on the null space, give 𝛃β a flat prior, and the penalized estimate is the posterior mode (Definition 39.4.1); with 𝛌λ fixed the posterior of 𝛄γ is normal with covariance σ2A−1 by Proposition 32.6.1(a). That covariance produces the bands in this chapter’s figures: the Wahba–Nychka bands of Theorem 43.6.1, with their caveat that they are honest about bias averaged over the curve, not pointwise, and condition on 𝛌λ as though it were known. Treating 𝛕τ2 and σ2 as unknown too gives a fully Bayesian model, sampled as in Section 39.4 (Definition 39.4.2), whose advantage is precisely that it propagates the uncertainty in 𝛌λ into the bands instead of ignoring it.

Warning (Improper priors on variance components). The prior Eq. 44.5.5 is improper on N(Pj), which is harmless: that part is a fixed effect with a flat prior. The prior on τj2 is not harmless. An inverse-gamma prior with both parameters tending to zero, or a flat prior on τj2, can produce an improper posterior, and the symptom in a sampler is not a crash but a chain that drifts towards zero variance and never settles (Proposition 39.5.1). Use a proper prior whose scale is set on the scale of the data, and check the sampler, never the fit alone.

44.5.5. Exercises

A. Check your understanding

Exercise (A1)

For each of the following, give the design block and the penalty: (i) a random slope of a covariate u within K clusters; (ii) a linear effect that is not to be penalized at all; (iii) a smooth term that is to be penalized towards a straight line rather than towards a constant.

B. Practice

Exercise (B1)

Take one term with Z=D the n×K matrix of cluster indicators and P=I. Show that the penalized estimate of the cluster effects is γ^k=mkr¯k/(mk+λ), where mk is the cluster size and r¯k the mean residual in cluster k, and identify this with Theorem 32.3.1 for the one-way random effects model of Definition 32.1.1.

Solution. With the other effects fixed the criterion is ∑k∑i∈k(ri−γk)2+λ∑kγk2, which separates over k and gives γ^k=mkr¯k/(mk+λ). With λ=σ2/τ2 that is r¯kmkτ2/(mkτ2+σ2), the shrinkage factor of Theorem 32.3.1 for the one-way model.

Exercise (B2)

Write the generalized cross-validation criterion Definition 29.3.2 for a STAR fit as 𝛌GCV(λ)=n‖y−Sy‖2/(n−trS)2. Show that it is minimized where σ2 of Eq. 44.5.4 is stationary in a particular sense, and explain why the two criteria nevertheless disagree in general.

C. Going deeper

Exercise (C1)

The updates Eq. 44.5.4 are a fixed-point iteration, not a descent method, and they are not guaranteed to converge from every start. Construct a two-term model in which the restricted likelihood has a stationary point at τ12=0 (the term is smoothed away), verify that this is a fixed point of Eq. 44.5.4, and explain how a run that lands there should be reported.

Solution. Let the first term be a P-spline in a covariate with no effect and the second anything. If the data show no roughness in that term, 𝛄𝛄γ^1⊤P1γ^1→0 while df1→m1, so Eq. 44.5.4 sends τ12→0 and λ1→∞, and there the update reproduces itself because the numerator stays zero. This is the boundary of the parameter space, where Theorem 32.5.2 says the usual asymptotics for a test of τ12=0 fail; the honest report is “the term was smoothed to its null space”, not “significant at 0 degrees of freedom”.
