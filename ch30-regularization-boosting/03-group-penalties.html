30.3  Group penalties

A factor with k levels enters a linear model as k−1 indicator columns (Section 8.5, Definition 8.5.1), and a smooth effect as several basis columns. The question is whether the factor matters. The lasso on the indicator columns can keep some levels and drop others, and its answer depends on the coding. Yuan and Lin (2006) proposed a penalty that treats a block of columns as a unit.

30.3.1. The group lasso

Partition the columns of X into groups g=1,…,G, with Xg the n×pg block of group g and bg the matching block of coefficients, so that Xb=∑gXgbg.

Definition 30.3.1 (Group lasso)

For weights wg>0, the group lasso criterion is

Q(b)=12‖y−∑g=1GXgbg‖2+λ∑g=1Gwg‖bg‖,
(30.3.1)

with Euclidean norms ‖bg‖, not squared. The usual weights are wg=pg. The columns of each group are usually centred and orthonormalized within the group before fitting, so that Xg⊤Xg=Ipg. Different groups are not made orthogonal to each other.

With singleton groups and unit weights this is the lasso. In general it acts like the lasso across groups and like ridge within them: ‖bg‖ has a corner only at bg=0, so a group is either absent or present with, typically, all its coefficients nonzero.

Proposition 30.3.1 (Group lasso and block soft thresholding)

  • (a) A minimizer of Eq. 30.3.1 exists, and all minimizers have the same fitted vector.

  • (b) (KKT conditions.) 𝛃β^ is a minimizer iff, with 𝛃r=y−Xβ^, for every group g,

    𝛃𝛃𝛃𝛃Xg⊤r=λwgβ^g‖β^g‖if β^g≠0,‖Xg⊤r‖≤λwgif β^g=0.
    (30.3.2)

  • (c) Let λ>0. Then 𝛃β^=0 iff λ≥λmax=maxg‖Xg⊤y‖/wg.

  • (d) (Orthonormal groups.) If Xg⊤Xh=Ipg for g=h and 0 for g≠h, the unique minimizer is block soft thresholding of zg=Xg⊤y:

    𝛃β^g=(1−λwg‖zg‖)+zg.
    (30.3.3)

  • (e) (Block update; compare Proposition 30.2.2.) If Xg⊤Xg=Ipg, then minimizing Eq. 30.3.1 over bg with the other blocks fixed gives Eq. 30.3.3 with zg replaced by sg=Xg⊤r(g), where r(g)=y−∑h≠gXhbh is the partial residual.

Proof.

  • (a) The penalty is convex and continuous. Since ∑g‖bg‖≥‖b‖, it is at least (mingwg)‖b‖. Apply Theorem 30.1.1(a)(ii) and (b).

  • (b) Combine Lemma 30.1.2 with Lemma 30.1.3, using the groups as the blocks.

  • (c) At 𝛃β^=0, the residual is y, and Eq. 30.3.2 says that ‖Xg⊤y‖≤λwg for every g. If this holds, 0 is a minimizer. Every minimizer then has fitted vector 0 and, by Theorem 30.1.1(b), penalty 0, so it is 0. If it fails, 0 is not a minimizer.

  • (d) Under the assumption, ‖y−Xb‖2=‖y‖2−2∑gzg⊤bg+∑g‖bg‖2. So Eq. 30.3.1 separates into the problems minbg{12‖zg−bg‖2+λwg‖bg‖}, each strictly convex. Let c=λwg and let bg be given by Eq. 30.3.3. If ‖zg‖≤c, then bg=0 and ‖zg−0‖≤c. If ‖zg‖>c, then bg=(‖zg‖−c)zg/‖zg‖≠0 and zg−bg=czg/‖zg‖=cbg/‖bg‖. In both cases the one-group version of Eq. 30.3.2 holds.

  • (e) As a function of bg, the criterion is 12‖r(g)−Xgbg‖2+λwg‖bg‖ plus a constant. With Xg⊤Xg=I, this is the one-group problem of (d) with zg replaced by sg.

Block soft thresholding keeps the direction of the least squares vector zg and reduces its length by λwg: the lasso rule applied to a length. The weights come from (c): for pure noise and orthonormal groups, ‖Xg⊤y‖2/σ2∼χ2(pg) has mean pg, so without pg large groups would enter merely for being large (Exercise (A1)). Block coordinate descent with update (e) has every limit point a minimizer because the penalty is separable across blocks (Tseng 2001), so it converges when the minimizer is unique; without orthonormality the block problem has no closed form (Exercise (C1)).

30.3.2. Factors as groups

Orthonormalizing within groups also makes the group lasso independent of how a factor is coded.

Proposition 30.3.2 (Invariance to the coding of a group)

  • (a) Let Ug be pg×pg orthogonal matrices, and replace every Xg by XgUg. Then 𝛃β^ minimizes the original criterion iff the vector with blocks 𝛃Ug⊤β^g minimizes the new one. The fitted contributions 𝛃Xgβ^g are the same.

  • (b) Suppose each group is orthonormalized within itself, so that Xg is an orthonormal basis of a subspace Vg. Then the minimizers, expressed through the fitted contributions 𝛃Xgβ^g∈Vg, depend on the groups only through the subspaces Vg. In particular, for a factor they do not depend on the choice of reference level or of contrasts.

Proof.

  • (a) With bg=Ugcg, we have XgUgcg=Xgbg and ‖cg‖=‖bg‖. So the new criterion at c equals the old criterion at b, and b↔c is a bijection.

  • (b) Two orthonormal bases Xg and Xg′ of Vg satisfy Xg′=XgUg with Ug=Xg⊤Xg′ orthogonal. Apply (a). For a factor with k levels, the centred indicator columns of any reference coding, and the centred columns of any other full-rank contrast coding, span the same (k−1)-dimensional subspace: the vectors that are constant within levels and orthogonal to 1.

With orthonormalized groups ‖bg‖=‖Xgbg‖: the penalty is the total length of the groups’ fitted contributions, a coding-free quantity. Simon and Tibshirani (2012) argue for this version.

Example (Selecting factors)

The script simulates n=120 observations of four factors A,B,C,D (with 4, 3, 5 and 3 levels) and three numeric regressors; the response depends on A, weakly on B, and on x1. The group lasso path starts at λmax=6.876, and groups enter in the order x1, A, B, D. At λ=2.0 only A and x1 are selected, with 𝛃‖XAβ^A‖=2.834 and 𝛃‖Xx1β^x1‖=4.861. Another reference level gives the same fitted contributions to machine precision. The lasso on the raw indicator columns does not: at λ=6.0 its fit moves by 8.6 per cent of its length when the reference levels change. Along its path the columns of A enter at values of λ spread over a factor of 2.8, and it admits its first column of a null factor (C or D) at a residual sum of squares of 112.0, a worse fit than the 110.7 that the group lasso reaches with both null factors still excluded (Figure 30.3.1).

import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(3003)
n = 120
levels = {"A": 4, "B": 3, "C": 5, "D": 3}
factors = {f: rng.integers(0, k, size=n) for f, k in levels.items()}
numeric = rng.normal(size=(n, 3))
effect_A = np.array([0.0, 1.0, -0.5, 0.8])
effect_B = np.array([0.0, 0.3, -0.3])
y = effect_A[factors["A"]] + effect_B[factors["B"]] + 0.6 * numeric[:, 0] + rng.normal(size=n)
y -= y.mean()

def indicator_columns(codes, k, reference=0):
    """Indicator columns for the levels other than the reference level."""
    return np.column_stack([(codes == l).astype(float) for l in range(k) if l != reference])

def orthonormal_group(Z):
    """Centre the columns, then an orthonormal basis of their span (Z = Q R)."""
    Z = Z - Z.mean(axis=0)
    Q, _ = np.linalg.qr(Z)
    return Q

def build(reference=0):
    blocks = [orthonormal_group(indicator_columns(factors[f], k, reference))
              for f, k in levels.items()]
    blocks += [orthonormal_group(numeric[:, [j]]) for j in range(3)]
    return blocks

def group_lasso(blocks, y, lam, b=None, tol=1e-12, max_sweeps=100000):
    """(1/2)||y - sum_g X_g b_g||^2 + lam sum_g sqrt(p_g) ||b_g||, with X_g'X_g = I."""
    b = [np.zeros(Xg.shape[1]) for Xg in blocks] if b is None else [v.copy() for v in b]
    r = y - sum(Xg @ bg for Xg, bg in zip(blocks, b))
    for _ in range(max_sweeps):
        largest = 0.0
        for g, Xg in enumerate(blocks):
            s = Xg.T @ r + b[g]                        # block least squares on the partial residual
            w = np.sqrt(Xg.shape[1])
            norm_s = np.linalg.norm(s)
            new = max(0.0, 1 - lam * w / norm_s) * s if norm_s > 0 else 0 * s
            r += Xg @ (b[g] - new)
            largest = max(largest, np.max(np.abs(new - b[g])))
            b[g] = new
        if largest < tol:
            return b
    raise RuntimeError("block coordinate descent did not converge")

blocks = build()
names = list(levels) + ["x1", "x2", "x3"]
lam = 2.0
b = group_lasso(blocks, y, lam)
for name, Xg, bg in zip(names, blocks, b):
    print(f"{name}: {Xg.shape[1]} columns, ||X_g b_g|| = {np.linalg.norm(Xg @ bg):.3f}")

The sparse group lasso adds an ℓ1 term so that a selected group can have zero coefficients (Simon, Friedman, Hastie and Tibshirani, 2013).

30.3.3. Ordered coefficients: the fused lasso

When coefficients have a natural order (levels of an ordered factor, successive times), neighbours are expected to be similar. The fused lasso of Tibshirani, Saunders, Rosset, Zhu and Knight (2005) penalizes their differences:

Q(b)=12‖y−Xb‖2+λ1∑j=1p|bj|+λ2∑j=2p|bj−bj−1|.
(30.3.4)

It produces exact zeros and exact ties β^j=β^j−1, so the coefficient sequence is piecewise constant. The penalty is convex but not separable, so coordinate descent can stall at a tie. For an ordered factor, fusion merges adjacent levels the data cannot distinguish (Gertheiss and Tutz 2010). Exercise (B1) solves the smallest case.

30.3.4. Exercises

A. Check your understanding

Exercise (A1)

Under orthonormal groups and y∼Nn(0,σ2I), show that 𝛃P(β^g≠0)=P{χ2(pg)>λ2wg2/σ2}. With wg=1, compare these probabilities for pg=1 and pg=10 when λ=2σ. What do the weights pg achieve, and what do they not achieve?

Solution. By Eq. 30.3.3, 𝛃β^g≠0 iff ‖zg‖>λwg. Here zg=Xg⊤y∼Npg(0,σ2I), so ‖zg‖2/σ2∼χ2(pg). With wg=1 and λ=2σ, the probabilities are P{χ2(1)>4}=0.046 and P{χ2(10)>4}=0.947, so a large null group almost always enters. With wg=pg the threshold is 4pg, four times the mean of χ2(pg). This removes the gross imbalance, but it does not equalize the probabilities: they are 0.046 and P{χ2(10)>40}=1.7×10−5. Equal null entry probabilities would need thresholds at a common quantile of χ2(pg).

B. Practice

Exercise (B1)

Solve the fused lasso Eq. 30.3.4 with X=I2, λ1=0 and λ2=λ. Show that β^1=β^2=12(y1+y2) if |y1−y2|≤2λ. Otherwise, each estimate moves a distance λ from its observation towards the other.

Solution. The penalty is λ|b1−b2|=λ|a⊤b| with a=(1,−1)⊤. Its subgradients are λsa with s∈∂|⋅|(b1−b2). By Lemma 30.1.2, y1−b1=λs and y2−b2=−λs. If b1=b2=b, adding the equations gives b=12(y1+y2), and then s=(y1−y2)/(2λ) must lie in [−1,1]. If |y1−y2|>2λ, then b1≠b2, s=sign(b1−b2), and b1=y1−λs, b2=y2+λs. This is consistent when s=sign(y1−y2), since then b1−b2=y1−y2−2λs has the sign of s.

Exercise (B2)

Under an orthonormal design, compare the group lasso (one group of size 3, w=3) with the lasso on the same three columns, for z=(2,0.3,−0.2)⊤ and λ=1. Which coefficients are zero in each? Show that, under an orthonormal design, the group lasso never sets a single coordinate of a selected group to zero unless the corresponding zj=0.
