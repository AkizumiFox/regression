30.2  The elastic net

When several regressors are near copies of one another (repeated measurements, genes in one pathway), the lasso tends to keep one and drop the rest, and which one can change with a small perturbation of the data. With two identical columns its solution is not even unique (Exercise B1 of Section 27.4). Ridge spreads the coefficient evenly over the copies but never sets anything to zero. Zou and Hastie (2005) proposed using both penalties.

30.2.1. Definition and basic properties

Definition 30.2.1 (Elastic net)

For λ1,λ2≥0, the elastic net criterion is

Q(b)=12‖y−Xb‖2+λ1‖b‖1+12λ2‖b‖2,
(30.2.1)

and its minimizer 𝛃β^en is the (naive) elastic net estimate. An equivalent parameterization is λ1=αλ, λ2=(1−α)λ, with a mixing parameter α∈[0,1] and an overall level λ≥0.

With λ2=0 this is the lasso, and with λ1=0 ridge. Here the columns of X are centred with unit length and y is centred, so ρij=xi⊤xj is a sample correlation.

Proposition 30.2.1 (Elastic net: uniqueness, augmented data, grouping)

Let λ2>0.

  • (a) The criterion Eq. 30.2.1 has a unique minimizer, whatever the rank of X. It is characterized by

    𝛃𝛃xj⊤(y−Xβ^)−λ2β^j=λ1sign(β^j)  (β^j≠0),|xj⊤(y−Xβ^)|≤λ1  (β^j=0).
    (30.2.2)

  • (b) (Augmented data.) Let X∗=(Xλ2Ip) and y∗=(y0). Then 𝛃β^en is the lasso estimate, with parameter λ1, for the data (X∗,y∗). The matrix X∗ has full column rank p.

  • (c) (Orthonormal design.) If X⊤X=I, then β^jen=sign(zj)(|zj|−λ1)+/(1+λ2), with z=X⊤y.

  • (d) (Grouping effect.) If β^iβ^j>0, then

    𝛃|β^i−β^j|≤‖y−Xβ^‖‖xi−xj‖λ2≤‖y‖2(1−ρij)λ2.
    (30.2.3)

  • (e) (Identical columns.) If xi=xj, then β^i=β^j.

Proof.

  • (a) The penalty λ1‖b‖1+12λ2‖b‖2 is strictly convex, because ‖b‖2 is strictly convex and ‖b‖1 is convex. It is at least 12λ2‖b‖2, so Q→∞. Existence and uniqueness follow as in Theorem 30.1.1(a) and (c). By Lemma 30.1.2 applied to the whole penalty, with the gradient 𝛃λ2β^ of the smooth part moved to the left-hand side, and Lemma 30.1.3, the minimizer is characterized by Eq. 30.2.2.

  • (b) ‖y∗−X∗b‖2=‖y−Xb‖2+λ2‖b‖2. So 12‖y∗−X∗b‖2+λ1‖b‖1 is Eq. 30.2.1. The last p rows of X∗ are λ2Ip, which has rank p.

  • (c) With X⊤X=I, Eq. 30.2.1 equals ∑j{12(1+λ2)bj2−zjbj+λ1|bj|} plus a constant. Dividing the jth term by 1+λ2 gives 12{bj−zj/(1+λ2)}2+{λ1/(1+λ2)}|bj| plus a constant. By the univariate lasso solution (Theorem 27.4.2), this is minimized by soft thresholding zj/(1+λ2) at λ1/(1+λ2), which is the stated formula.

  • (d) Both coefficients are nonzero with a common sign s, so Eq. 30.2.2 gives xi⊤r−λ2β^i=λ1s=xj⊤r−λ2β^j, where 𝛃r=y−Xβ^. Subtracting, λ2(β^i−β^j)=(xi−xj)⊤r, and Cauchy–Schwarz gives the first inequality. For the second, ‖xi−xj‖2=2−2ρij for unit-length columns, and 𝛃12‖r‖2≤Q(β^)≤Q(0)=12‖y‖2.

  • (e) Swapping coordinates i and j of 𝛃β^ changes neither 𝛃Xβ^ nor the penalty. So the swapped vector is also a minimizer, and by uniqueness it equals 𝛃β^.

By (b), the elastic net is a lasso on n+p observations with a full-rank design, so it can select all p regressors even when p>n; a unique lasso solution has at most min(n,p) nonzero coefficients (Exercise (C1)). Part (d) is the grouping effect of Zou and Hastie (2005): as ρij→1, coefficients of the same sign are forced together at a rate set by λ2. No such bound holds for the lasso.

Example (Three near-copies of one signal)

The script simulates n=50 observations of p=10 regressors. The first three are noisy copies of a common variable, with pairwise correlations between 0.93 and 0.95, and each has coefficient 2 (on the unit-length scale). The other seven are independent noise. With λ1=0.8:

β^1 β^2 β^3 nonzero
lasso (λ2=0) 2.335 0.000 2.522 2
elastic net (λ2=1) 1.248 1.103 1.236 4
rescaled elastic net 2.496 2.207 2.471 4

The lasso drops the second copy. The elastic net keeps all three with similar coefficients (and one noise regressor, at 0.081). For the pair (1,2), with correlation 0.927, the spread 0.144 is well inside Eq. 30.2.3, whose middle term is 1.577 (residual norm 4.129) and right-hand side 2.532 (‖y‖=6.630). Along the lasso path (Figure 30.2.1) the second copy enters only after λ has fallen by a factor of about 26; along the elastic net path all three enter at the same grid value.

30.2.2. Computing it: coordinate descent

Cyclic coordinate descent, used for the lasso in Lemma 27.4.4, minimizes over one coordinate at a time, the others fixed, and sweeps until nothing changes.

Proposition 30.2.2 (Coordinate update)

Fix all coordinates of b except bj, and let r(j)=y−∑k≠jxkbk be the partial residual. The value of bj that minimizes Eq. 30.2.1 is

bj=sign(xj⊤r(j))(|xj⊤r(j)|−λ1)+‖xj‖2+λ2.
(30.2.4)

Proof. As a function of bj alone, Eq. 30.2.1 equals 12(‖xj‖2+λ2)bj2−bjxj⊤r(j)+λ1|bj| plus a constant. Divided by ‖xj‖2+λ2, this is a univariate lasso problem of the form in the proof of Proposition 30.2.1(c). Its solution is Eq. 30.2.4.

Because the nonsmooth part is separable, every limit point of the iterates is a minimizer (Tseng, 2001; cited, not proved), so the iterates converge when the minimizer is unique, as for the elastic net with λ2>0. For a coupling penalty such as ∑j|bj−bj−1|, coordinate descent can stall. Whole paths are computed on a decreasing grid of λ, each fit starting from the last (Friedman, Hastie, Höfling and Tibshirani, 2007; Friedman, Hastie and Tibshirani, 2010). The listing works through the Gram matrix, as the next proposition requires.

import numpy as np

def soft(z, lam):
    return np.sign(z) * np.maximum(np.abs(z) - lam, 0.0)

def cd_quadratic(G, c, lam1, b=None, tol=1e-12, max_sweeps=100000):
    """Minimize (1/2) b'Gb - c'b + lam1 ||b||_1 by cyclic coordinate descent."""
    p = len(c)
    b = np.zeros(p) if b is None else b.copy()
    for _ in range(max_sweeps):
        largest = 0.0
        for j in range(p):
            old = b[j]
            partial = c[j] - G[j] @ b + G[j, j] * old      # c_j - sum_{k != j} G_jk b_k
            b[j] = soft(partial, lam1) / G[j, j]
            largest = max(largest, abs(b[j] - old))
        if largest < tol:
            return b
    raise RuntimeError("coordinate descent did not converge")

def elastic_net(X, y, lam1, lam2, b=None):
    """The (naive) elastic net: (1/2)||y - Xb||^2 + lam1 ||b||_1 + (lam2/2)||b||^2."""
    G = X.T @ X + lam2 * np.eye(X.shape[1])
    return cd_quadratic(G, X.T @ y, lam1, b)

rng = np.random.default_rng(3002)
n, p = 50, 10
z = rng.normal(size=n)
X = rng.normal(size=(n, p))
X[:, :3] = z[:, None] + 0.3 * rng.normal(size=(n, 3))   # three near-copies of one signal
X -= X.mean(axis=0)
X /= np.linalg.norm(X, axis=0)                          # centred, unit-length columns
beta = np.r_[2.0, 2.0, 2.0, np.zeros(p - 3)]
y = X @ beta + 0.5 * rng.normal(size=n)
y -= y.mean()

lam1 = 0.8
b_lasso = elastic_net(X, y, lam1, 0.0)
b_naive = elastic_net(X, y, lam1, 1.0)
print("lasso         ", np.round(b_lasso[:5], 3))
print("naive EN      ", np.round(b_naive[:5], 3))
print("rescaled EN   ", np.round(2.0 * b_naive[:5], 3))     # (1 + lam2) * naive

30.2.3. Double shrinkage and the rescaled elastic net

By Proposition 30.2.1(c), under an orthonormal design the elastic net soft-thresholds and then divides by 1+λ2: surviving coefficients are shrunk twice. Zou and Hastie (2005) proposed reporting 𝛃(1+λ2)β^en instead, which in general is a lasso with a modified Gram matrix.

Proposition 30.2.3 (The rescaled elastic net)

Let the columns of X have unit length and let λ2>0. Then 𝛃𝛃β~=(1+λ2)β^en is the unique minimizer of

12c⊤Σλ2c−y⊤Xc+λ1‖c‖1,Σλ2=X⊤X+λ2I1+λ2.
(30.2.5)

Proof. Up to the constant 12y⊤y, Eq. 30.2.1 is 12b⊤(X⊤X+λ2I)b−y⊤Xb+λ1‖b‖1. Substitute b=c/(1+λ2) and multiply by 1+λ2>0. The result is Eq. 30.2.5. The substitution is a bijection, so minimizers correspond, and the minimizer of Eq. 30.2.1 is unique by Proposition 30.2.1(a).

The lasso has X⊤X in place of Σλ2, whose correlations are divided by 1+λ2: the rescaled elastic net is a lasso computed as if the regressors were less correlated. The rescaling is a heuristic; in Example (Three near-copies of one signal), where the true coefficients are 2, the naive estimates are about half that and the rescaled ones somewhat too large. The glmnet software (Friedman, Hastie and Tibshirani, 2010) fits the naive criterion.

30.2.4. Exercises

A. Check your understanding

Exercise (A1)

Under an orthonormal design with z=X⊤y=(3,0.5,−1.2)⊤, λ1=1 and λ2=1, compute the lasso, ridge, naive elastic net and rescaled elastic net estimates.

Solution. Lasso (λ=1): (2,0,−0.2). Ridge (λ=1): z/2=(1.5,0.25,−0.6). Naive elastic net: the lasso divided by 2, (1,0,−0.1). Rescaled: multiply by 1+λ2=2, which gives the lasso, (2,0,−0.2).

B. Practice

Exercise (B1)

Show that if β^iβ^j<0, then |β^i+β^j|≤‖y‖2(1+ρij)/λ2. Interpret this for two regressors with correlation close to −1.

Solution. With signs s and −s, Eq. 30.2.2 gives xi⊤r−λ2β^i=λ1s and xj⊤r−λ2β^j=−λ1s. Adding, λ2(β^i+β^j)=(xi+xj)⊤r, and ‖xi+xj‖2=2+2ρij. Bound ‖r‖≤‖y‖ as before. If ρij≈−1, then xj≈−xi, and the bound forces β^j≈−β^i. The two regressors are treated as one regressor with a sign flip.

Exercise (B2)

Let X=[x,x] with ‖x‖=1. Find the elastic net estimate in closed form and compare it with the ridge and lasso estimates.

C. Going deeper

Exercise (C1)

Suppose p>n and the lasso estimate 𝛃β^ is unique with active set A. Use the KKT conditions to show that the columns XA are linearly independent, and deduce that |A|≤n. (Hint: if XAd=0 with d≠0, move along 𝛃β^+τ(d,0) and use that the fit and the ℓ1 norm are constant among minimizers.)
