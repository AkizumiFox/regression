Projections: The Geometry of Least Squares

Least squares chooses the point of a subspace that is closest to the data. Everything else in this chapter follows from that one statement: the normal equations, why fitted values are unique when coefficients are not, the orthogonal decompositions behind every sum of squares, the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell theorem, R2 as a squared cosine, leverage, generalized least squares, and how the projection should actually be computed.

What you need. Chapter 1 (rank, inverses, eigenvalues), Chapter 5 (the linear model and least squares). Random vectors (Chapter 2) appear only in a few remarks.
