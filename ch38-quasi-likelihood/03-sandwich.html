38.3  Sandwich covariance estimation

Theorem 38.2.2 left an asymmetry: the estimating equations are unbiased as soon as the mean model is right, while the identity that turns An=ϕ−1X⊤WX into a covariance needs the variance model too. When the latter fails, the covariance of 𝛃β^ becomes a product of three matrices instead of one, and the middle factor can be estimated from the residuals even though its n ingredients cannot.

38.3.1. The asymptotic distribution

Throughout 𝛃β0 is the true parameter, 𝛃ηi0=x(i)⊤β0, μi0=h(ηi0), and σi2=Var(Yi) is not assumed equal to ϕV(μi0)/wi. Write a(η)=h′(η)V{h(η)},An=1ϕX⊤WX=1ϕ∑i=1nwia(ηi0)h′(ηi0)x(i)x(i)⊤, and let Bn be the matrix Eq. 38.2.3. The conditions are

  • (Q1) the Yi are independent with EYi=μi0 for a 𝛃β0 interior to the admissible set, p is fixed and rank(X)=p;
  • (Q2) ‖x(i)‖≤C and 0<wmin≤wi≤wmax<∞;

38.3.2. Model-based or robust

The two covariance estimates answer different questions. Under the log link with V(μ)=μ and wi=1, where Wii=μi and a(ηi)=1, An−1BnAn−1=(∑iμix(i)x(i)⊤)−1(∑iσi2x(i)x(i)⊤)(∑iμix(i)x(i)⊤)−1, against the model-based ϕ(∑iμix(i)x(i)⊤)−1. Only the shape of the dependence on μ matters, since a constant factor is absorbed into ϕ: a quasi-Poisson analysis is right about the standard errors whenever the true variance is proportional to the mean, whatever the constant, and wrong for any other shape.

Example (Coverage when the variance function is right and when it is wrong)

A quasi-Poisson model log⁡μi=β0+β1xi was fitted to data simulated with the same mean and two true variances, Var(Yi)=μi+2μi2 (quadratic) and Var(Yi)=9μi (linear), over 3000 replicates at each of seven sample sizes. Figure 38.3.1 records the coverage of nominal 95% intervals for β1 from four standard errors: Poisson with ϕ=1, model-based scaled by ϕ^, the empirical sandwich Eq. 38.3.2, and a leverage-corrected sandwich described below.

Ignoring the dispersion is fatal in both panels and does not improve with n: 0.334 at n=20 and 0.323 at n=1280 in the quadratic case, 0.513 and 0.482 in the linear case. A linear true variance makes the specification correct and the ϕ^-scaled interval already good at n=20, covering 0.919 against the sandwich’s 0.880; a quadratic one stops it improving, at 0.914 for n=1280, while the sandwich climbs to 0.941, buying correctness in the limit at the price of noise in small samples, as in Example (Coverage in a skewed design).

import numpy as np

BETA = np.array([1.5, 1.0])
HALF = 2.0            # the regressor runs over [-HALF, HALF]

def quasi_poisson(X, y, steps=25):
    """IRLS for the quasi-score equations with a log link and V(mu) = mu."""
    beta = np.zeros(X.shape[1])
    beta[0] = np.log(max(y.mean(), 0.5))
    for _ in range(steps):
        mu = np.exp(X @ beta)
        z = X @ beta + (y - mu) / mu
        beta = np.linalg.solve((X.T * mu) @ X, (X.T * mu) @ z)
    return beta, np.exp(X @ beta)

def slope_variances(X, y):
    """The four estimates of Var(b1): naive, phi-scaled, sandwich, leverage-corrected."""
    beta, mu = quasi_poisson(X, y)
    n, p = X.shape
    bread = np.linalg.inv((X.T * mu) @ X)
    root = np.sqrt(mu)[:, None] * X
    hat = np.sum(root * (root @ bread), axis=1)
    r2 = (y - mu) ** 2
    phi = np.sum(r2 / mu) / (n - p)
    meat = lambda w: bread @ ((X * w[:, None]).T @ X) @ bread
    return beta[1], np.array([bread[1, 1], phi * bread[1, 1],
                              meat(r2)[1, 1], meat(r2 / (1 - hat) ** 2)[1, 1]])

def coverage(n, quadratic, reps, rng, kappa=0.5, phi=9.0):
    """Coverage of nominal 95% normal intervals for b1 over `reps` simulated data sets."""
    x = np.linspace(-HALF, HALF, n)
    X = np.column_stack([np.ones(n), x])
    mu = np.exp(X @ BETA)
    shape = np.full(n, kappa) if quadratic else mu / (phi - 1.0)
    hit = np.zeros(4)
    for _ in range(reps):
        y = rng.negative_binomial(shape, shape / (shape + mu)).astype(float)
        b1, var = slope_variances(X, y)
        hit += np.abs(b1 - BETA[1]) <= 1.96 * np.sqrt(var)
    return hit / reps

rng = np.random.default_rng(3821)
names = ["Poisson", "phi-scaled", "sandwich", "corrected"]
for n in (25, 100, 400):
    quad = coverage(n, True, 300, rng)
    lin = coverage(n, False, 300, rng)
    print(f"n = {n:4d}  quadratic variance " + "  ".join(
        f"{k} {v:.3f}" for k, v in zip(names, quad)))
    print(f"          linear variance    " + "  ".join(
        f"{k} {v:.3f}" for k, v in zip(names, lin)))

38.3.3. The dispersion, the quasi-deviance and tests

The model-based covariance needs ϕ, and so does any F test; neither 𝛃β^ nor the sandwich Eq. 38.3.2 does. Since ϕ cancels from Eq. 38.1.1 and scales An by ϕ−1, it is orthogonal to 𝛃β: estimating it multiplies the asymptotic covariance of 𝛃β^ by a consistent factor rather than inflating it.

Proposition 38.3.2 (Estimating the dispersion, and quasi-deviance tests)

Assume Eq. 38.1.2 with the mean model correct.

  • (a) E[wi(Yi−μi0)2/V(μi0)]=ϕ for every i, so the Pearson estimator ϕ^P=X2/(n−p) with X2=∑iwi(yi−μ^i)2/V(μ^i) — the statistic Eq. 34.5.5 — is consistent for ϕ under (Q1)–(Q5). The divisor n−p is an analogy with s2 (Theorem 5.6.1), exact only in the normal linear model.

  • (b) The deviance estimator 𝛍ϕ^D=D(y,μ^)/(n−p), with D the quasi-deviance Eq. 38.2.2, is not in general consistent for ϕ. The two agree only so far as a third-order remainder is negligible: large binomial groups and large Poisson means, not small counts. Only ϕ^P has expectation exactly ϕ at the true mean.

  • (c) For nested mean models C(X0)⊆C(X) with rank(X)−rank(X0)=q, fitted with the same V, the quasi-F statistic is

    𝛍𝛍F={D(y,μ~)−D(y,μ^)}/qϕ^P.
    (38.3.3)

    Under the reduced model and (Q1)–(Q5), qF→χ2(q), so referring F to F(q,n−p) is asymptotically correct and is the usual small-sample convention. If the variance specification is wrong the limit fails and the Wald statistic built from Eq. 38.3.2 should be used instead.

  • (d) No convention makes 𝛃−2Q(β^) a log-likelihood, so Definition 29.2.2 does not apply. The criterion 𝛍QAIC=D(y,μ^)/ϕ^+2p, with ϕ^ fixed at its value in the largest model, is in common use, but it is a convention, not a theorem. Cross-validation (Definition 29.3.1) needs no likelihood, and the analogue for correlated responses, the QIC of Chapter 40, rests on the same convention.

Proof.

  • (a) Under Eq. 38.1.2, E(Yi−μi0)2=ϕV(μi0)/wi, which is the identity. The Ti=wi(Yi−μi0)2/V(μi0) are independent with mean ϕ and bounded variances, so n−1∑iTi→ϕ by Chebyshev; replacing μi0 by μ^i costs op(1), by Step 5 of the proof of Theorem 38.3.1 with x(i)x(i)⊤ replaced by 1, and n/(n−p)→1.

  • (b) Each term of Eq. 38.2.2 is 2wi∫μ^iyi(yi−t)V(t)−1dt. Expanding 1/V(t) about t=yi and integrating gives wi(yi−μ^i)2/V(yi)+O(wi|yi−μ^i|3), the calculation recorded after Eq. 34.5.5: a Pearson term with V(yi) in place of V(μ^i). The two estimators therefore differ by the remainder and by the gap between those denominators, both negligible when |yi−μ^i| is small relative to μi and neither otherwise; there is no exact unbiasedness to appeal to, since Edi≠ϕ in general.

  • (c) By Eq. 38.2.2 the difference is 𝛃𝛃2ϕ{Q(β^)−Q(β~)}. The proof of Proposition 34.5.3 used the log-likelihood only through the quadratic expansion Eq. 34.5.3, whose ingredients are asymptotic normality of the score and the identity between its variance and minus the expected Hessian. Under Eq. 38.1.2, Step 1 of the proof of Theorem 38.3.1 supplies the first and Theorem 38.2.2(d) the second, with An in both roles, so that argument goes through word for word with Q in place of ℓ. Hence 𝛍𝛍{D(y,μ~)−D(y,μ^)}/ϕ→χ2(q); part (a) lets ϕ^P replace ϕ, and qF(q,n−p)→χ2(q) makes the F reference asymptotically equivalent.

  • (d) is a definition and a warning, not a claim.

Example (Four standard errors for the strike data)

Continue Example (Strike durations). The Pearson statistic is 2393.5 on 60 degrees of freedom, so ϕ^P=39.89, against ϕ^D=37.22 from the quasi-deviance. The four standard errors for the production coefficient −7.680 are

covariance standard error t
Poisson, ϕ=1 0.406 −18.92
model-based, ϕ^P 2.564 −3.00
empirical sandwich 2.573 −2.98
leverage-corrected sandwich 2.896 −2.65

The Poisson standard error is too small by a factor of about six. More interesting are the second and third rows: the sandwich exceeds the model-based value by a factor of only 1.004, which is evidence for the quasi-Poisson variance function, so the model-based standard error is valid and slightly more efficient. The fourth row divides each squared residual by (1−hii)2, with hii the leverages of the weighted hat matrix of Definition 34.5.2; the largest is 0.192, enough to matter at n=62.

import numpy as np
import statsmodels.api as sm

data = sm.datasets.strikes.load_pandas().data
y = data["duration"].to_numpy(float)          # length of the strike, in days
x = data["iprod"].to_numpy(float)             # unanticipated industrial production
X = np.column_stack([np.ones(len(y)), x])
n, p = X.shape

def quasi_irls(X, y, V, steps=60):
    """Solve the quasi-score equations for a log link and variance function V."""
    beta = np.zeros(X.shape[1])
    beta[0] = np.log(y.mean())
    for _ in range(steps):
        eta = X @ beta
        mu = np.exp(eta)
        W = mu ** 2 / V(mu)                   # working weight (dmu/deta)^2 / V(mu)
        z = eta + (y - mu) / mu               # working response
        beta = np.linalg.solve((X.T * W) @ X, (X.T * W) @ z)
    return beta

beta_lin = quasi_irls(X, y, lambda m: m)              # V(mu) = mu
mu = np.exp(X @ beta_lin)
pearson = np.sum((y - mu) ** 2 / mu)                          # X^2 with V(mu) = mu
phi_pearson = pearson / (n - p)
W = mu                                        # log link with V(mu) = mu: W_ii = mu_i
bread = np.linalg.inv((X.T * W) @ X)          # (X' W X)^{-1}
root = np.sqrt(W)[:, None] * X
hat = np.sum(root * (root @ bread), axis=1)   # leverages of the weighted hat matrix
resid = y - mu

cov_model = bread                             # phi = 1: the Poisson standard errors
cov_quasi = phi_pearson * bread               # phi estimated from the same fit
cov_sand = bread @ ((X * resid[:, None] ** 2).T @ X) @ bread
cov_corr = bread @ ((X * (resid ** 2 / (1 - hat) ** 2)[:, None]).T @ X) @ bread

for name, C in [("Poisson", cov_model), ("quasi", cov_quasi),
                ("sandwich", cov_sand), ("corrected", cov_corr)]:
    se = np.sqrt(np.diag(C))
    print(f"{name:10s} slope {beta_lin[1]:8.3f}   se {se[1]:6.3f}   t {beta_lin[1] / se[1]:7.2f}")
print(f"largest leverage {hat.max():.4f}, sum {hat.sum():.4f}")

38.3.4. Corrections, clusters and resampling

Three qualifications finish the section.

Small samples. The empirical sandwich is biased downwards, for the reason Proposition 21.4.1(a) identified in the linear model, and the remedy is the same: divide the ith squared residual by 1−hii or by (1−hii)2, with hii the diagonal of W^1/2X(X⊤W^X)−1X⊤W^1/2, giving the analogues of HC2 and HC3 of Definition 21.4.1. The second is the “corrected” line of Example (Coverage when the variance function is right and when it is wrong), better than the plain sandwich at n=80 in the quadratic panel, 0.906 against 0.891; Kauermann and Carroll (2001) give the leading bias term. The middle of Eq. 38.3.2 is itself an average with very unequal weights, so it behaves like a variance estimate on far fewer than n−p degrees of freedom (Example (One household carries the standard error)).

Clusters. If the observations come in G independent groups with arbitrary within-group dependence, the middle of Eq. 38.3.2 becomes ∑g=1G(∑i∈gc^i(yi−μ^i)x(i))(∑i∈gc^i(yi−μ^i)x(i))⊤, the cluster-robust form Eq. 33.2.3 with the quasi-score’s coefficients in place of the rows of X. Its consistency needs G→∞, by Step 5 above with the cluster in place of the observation. The quasi-score is then no longer optimal, because the within-group correlations are ignored; Chapter 40 puts a generalized estimating equation in its place.

Resampling. The bootstrap of Chapter 23 applies unchanged: whole observations (Proposition 23.1.5), the wild bootstrap (Definition 23.2.1), or whole clusters (Exercise (C1)) for small G.

38.3.5. Exercises

A. Check your understanding

Exercise (A1)

Under a log link and V(μ)=μ with wi≡1, suppose the true variances are σi2=τμi for an unknown τ>0. Show that the sandwich and the model-based covariance have the same probability limit, and identify what ϕ^P estimates. Repeat for σi2=τμi2 and show that the two then differ unless all μi are equal.

Exercise (A2)

Verify from Eq. 38.3.2 that the empirical sandwich does not change if V is replaced by cV for a constant c>0, and explain why this is to be expected.

B. Practice

Exercise (B1)

Take an intercept-only quasi-Poisson model, log⁡μi=β for all i. Show that μ^i=y¯, that the model-based variance of β^ is ϕ^P/(ny¯), and that the sandwich variance is ∑i(yi−y¯)2/(ny¯)2. Show that the two agree exactly when ϕ^P is computed with divisor n rather than n−p.

Exercise (B2)

Let Var(Yi)=σi2 and consider the quasi-score built from a working variance function V. Using Proposition 38.2.3 with the true variances, show that An−1BnAn−1 — the asymptotic covariance of Theorem 38.3.1(a) — is never smaller, in the nonnegative definite order, than the inverse Godambe information (∑ih′(ηi)2σi−2x(i)x(i)⊤)−1 of the best linear estimating function, and that the two agree exactly when σi2∝V(μi)/wi.

Solution. Repeat the proof of Proposition 38.2.3 with Σ=diag(σi2) in place of diag{ϕV(μi)/wi}; only its last display used the specification. The quasi-score built from V is a linear estimating function with ai=wih′(ηi)x(i)/{ϕV(μi)}, so by Theorem 38.2.2(c),(e) its Godambe information is AnBn−1An, at most the maximum K⊤K=∑ih′(ηi)2σi−2x(i)x(i)⊤; inverting reverses the order. Equality needs coefficients proportional to h′(ηi)x(i)/σi2, that is wi/V(μi)∝1/σi2.

C. Going deeper

Exercise (C1)

A large discrepancy between ϕ^P(X⊤W^X)−1 and Eq. 38.3.2 is evidence that the variance specification is wrong. Propose a test statistic from the difference of the two matrices, say tr{(X⊤W^X)Σ^rob}−pϕ^P, argue informally that its mean is about zero when the specification is correct, and say what stands in the way of a usable null distribution. (This is White’s information matrix test; compare Exercise (B3).)
