2.6  Moment generating functions and independence

Means and covariance matrices summarize a distribution. They do not determine it, and they cannot certify independence. For that we need a transform of the whole distribution. This section collects the facts that Chapter 3 and Chapter 4 use.

Definition 2.6.1 (Moment generating function)

The moment generating function of a p×1 random vector Y is MY(t)=Eexp⁡(t⊤Y),t∈Rp, where the expectation may be +∞. We say that MY exists if it is finite for all t in some neighbourhood {‖t‖<h} of the origin, h>0.

Theorem 2.6.1 (Properties of moment generating functions)

Suppose MY exists.

  • (a) Uniqueness. If MV also exists and MY=MV on a neighbourhood of 0, then Y and V have the same distribution.

  • (b) Moments. All moments of Y are finite. The gradient of MY at 0 is E(Y), and its Hessian at 0 is E(YY⊤). The Hessian of log⁡MY at 0 is Cov(Y).

  • (c) Affine maps. For a constant k×p matrix A and k×1 vector b, MAY+b(s)=exp⁡(s⊤b)MY(A⊤s).

  • (d) Independence. Partition Y=(Y1⊤,Y2⊤)⊤ and t=(t1⊤,t2⊤)⊤ conformably. Then Y1 and Y2 are independent iff MY(t)=MY1(t1)MY2(t2) for all t in a neighbourhood of 0.

Proof. For (a), the scalar case is a theorem of analysis: a moment generating function that is finite near the origin extends analytically, which pins down the characteristic function and hence the distribution (Billingsley 1995). For vectors, fix a. For small |s|, Ma⊤Y(s)=MY(sa)=MV(sa)=Ma⊤V(s), so a⊤Y and a⊤V have the same distribution by the scalar case, and Proposition 2.6.2 below finishes the argument. For (b), finiteness near 0 implies Eexp⁡(h′|Yi|)<∞ for some h′>0 and each i, so every polynomial in the components is integrable and differentiation under the expectation is justified. Then ∂M/∂ti=E(Yiet⊤Y) and ∂2M/∂ti∂tj=E(YiYjet⊤Y). At t=0 these are E(Yi) and E(YiYj). For K=log⁡M, the chain rule gives ∇2K=M−1∇2M−M−2(∇M)(∇M)⊤, which at 0, where M=1, is E(YY⊤)−μμ⊤=Cov(Y). For (c), Eexp⁡(s⊤(AY+b))=es⊤bEexp⁡((A⊤s)⊤Y). For (d), independence gives E(et1⊤Y1et2⊤Y2)=E(et1⊤Y1)E(et2⊤Y2). Conversely, let V1 and V2 be independent with the distributions of Y1 and Y2. The vector (V1⊤,V2⊤)⊤ has moment generating function MY1(t1)MY2(t2), which by hypothesis equals MY near 0. By (a) it has the same joint distribution as Y, so Y1 and Y2 are independent.

The next fact explains why Chapter 3 can define the multivariate normal through linear combinations.

Proposition 2.6.2 (Distributions are determined by linear combinations)

If a⊤Y and a⊤V have the same distribution for every a∈Rp, then Y and V have the same distribution.

Proof. The characteristic function ϕY(t)=Eexp⁡(it⊤Y) exists for every random vector and determines its distribution (Billingsley 1995). Since ϕY(t)=ϕt⊤Y(1)=ϕt⊤V(1)=ϕV(t) for every t, the two distributions coincide.

If Y1 and Y2 are independent, so are g(Y1) and h(Y2) for measurable g and h. Independence of statistics is usually proved this way. For normal vectors, Theorem 3.4.1 reduces the independence of two blocks to a zero cross-covariance, and Theorem 4.4.1 and Theorem 4.4.2 build on that.

Independence of several vectors means that the joint distribution factors into all the marginals, not merely that each pair is independent.

Example (Pairwise but not mutually independent)

Let S1 and S2 be independent, each equal to ±1 with probability 12, and put S3=S1S2. Each Sj is ±1 with probability 12. Each pair is independent: for instance, Pr(S1=s,S3=u)=Pr(S1=s,S2=su)=14 for all signs s,u. So Cov(S)=I3. But the three are not mutually independent, since S3 is a function of the other two. A third-order moment shows the failure directly: E(S1S2S3)=1, whereas mutual independence would force it to be 0. No condition on the covariance matrix can detect this, because covariances involve only pairs.

2.6.1. Exercises

A. Check your understanding

Exercise (A1)

Find the moment generating function of the multinomial count vector of Example (Multinomial counts), and recover its mean and covariance matrix from Theorem 2.6.1(b).

B. Practice

Exercise (B1)

Let S1,…,Sk be independent random signs. For each nonempty A⊆{1,…,k} put TA=∏i∈ASi. Show that the 2k−1 variables TA are pairwise independent with covariance matrix I, and find a triple of them that is not mutually independent.
