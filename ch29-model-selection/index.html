Model Selection and Prediction

A fitted model is judged by how well it predicts responses it has not seen, and its error on the responses it was fitted to flatters it. For any fitting procedure the flattery is twice the covariance between the fit and the data, 2σ2p/n for least squares whether the model is right or wrong. Correcting for it gives Mallows’ Cp and AIC. BIC answers a different question, which model is most probable: it finds a true finite model with probability tending to one, while AIC predicts almost as well as the best candidate when no finite model is true. Cross-validation estimates prediction error with fewer assumptions, and for least squares and ridge it needs no refitting. The search over subsets is unstable, and every estimate and interval computed after it is biased by it, unless the inference is designed to survive the search.
