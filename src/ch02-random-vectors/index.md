# Random Vectors: Expectation and Covariance Operators

A linear model is a statement about the first two moments of a random
vector: its mean lies in a subspace and its covariance matrix has a given form. This
chapter builds the calculus for such statements. Expectation and covariance become
matrix operators that commute with linear maps, covariance matrices turn out to be
exactly the nonnegative definite matrices, and a singular covariance means the vector is
confined to a flat. The mean of a quadratic form needs only two moments; its
variance needs four. Partitioning a vector gives cross-covariances, Schur complements and
the best linear predictor, which foreshadow conditional distributions under normality.

**What you need.** [Chapter 1](../ch01-matrix-algebra/index.html) (trace, spectral theorem, nonnegative definite
matrices and their square roots, generalized inverses, partitioned matrices), and
probability at the level of expectations, variances and independence of random
variables.
