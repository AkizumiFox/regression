# Quadratic Forms and Their Distributions

Every sum of squares in a linear model is a quadratic form
\( \Y\T\A\Y \) in a normal vector, and every \( F \) and \( t \) statistic is a ratio built
from such forms. This chapter works out their laws: the noncentral
chi-squared, \( F \) and \( t \) distributions and the monotonicity of their tails; the
mean, variance and a canonical representation of an arbitrary quadratic form;
exactly when a quadratic form is chi-squared; when two forms, or a form and a
linear function, are independent; and Cochran's theorem, which says when a sum
of squares splits into independent chi-squared pieces.

**What you need.** [Chapter 1](../ch01-matrix-algebra/index.html) (spectral theorem, symmetric idempotent
matrices, trace), [Chapter 2](../ch02-random-vectors/index.html) (means and covariances of random
vectors) and [Chapter 3](../ch03-multivariate-normal/index.html) (the multivariate normal distribution
and its independence properties). Orthogonal projections appear here only as
symmetric idempotent matrices. [Chapter 6](../ch06-projections/index.html) develops their geometry.
