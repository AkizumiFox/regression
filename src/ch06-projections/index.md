# Projections: The Geometry of Least Squares

Least squares chooses the point of a subspace that is closest to
the data. Everything else in this chapter follows from that one statement:
the normal equations, why fitted values are unique when
coefficients are not, the orthogonal decompositions behind every sum of
squares, the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell theorem, \( R^2 \) as a squared cosine, leverage,
generalized least squares, and how the projection should actually be computed.

**What you need.** [Chapter 1](../ch01-matrix-algebra/index.html) (rank, inverses, eigenvalues),
[Chapter 5](../ch05-model-and-least-squares/index.html) (the linear model and least squares). Random
vectors ([Chapter 2](../ch02-random-vectors/index.html)) appear only in a few remarks.
