1.9  Idempotent matrices

A square matrix A is idempotent if A2=A. Applying it twice has the same effect as applying it once, which is how a projection behaves. Examples are I, 0, the centring matrix of Example (Sums of squares as quadratic forms), the products AG and GA for a generalized inverse G (Proposition 1.3.3(a)), and the non-symmetric (1100). In linear models, sums of squares are quadratic forms in symmetric idempotent matrices, and their degrees of freedom are ranks.

Proposition 1.9.1 (General idempotent matrices)

Let A be n×n and idempotent of rank r.

  • (a) Every eigenvalue of A is 0 or 1.

  • (b) rank(A)=tr(A).

  • (c) I−A is idempotent, A(I−A)=(I−A)A=0, C(I−A)=N(A), and rank(A)+rank(I−A)=n.

  • (d) An idempotent matrix that is nonsingular equals I.

  • (e) P−1AP is idempotent for nonsingular P, and A⊤ is idempotent.

Proof.

  • (a) If Ax=λx with x≠0, then λx=Ax=A2x=λ2x, so λ2=λ.
  • (b) If r=0 both sides vanish. Otherwise take a rank factorization A=BF (Theorem 1.2.1). Then BFBF=BF. Since B has full column rank and F has full row rank, B has a left inverse and F a right inverse (Section 1.3), and cancelling them gives FB=Ir. So tr(A)=tr(BF)=tr(FB)=r by Theorem 1.6.1.
  • (c) The first two claims are one-line expansions. C(I−A)⊆N(A) because A(I−A)=0, and v∈N(A) gives v=(I−A)v. The rank statement is then rank–nullity.
  • (d) Multiply A2=A by A−1.
  • (e) (P−1AP)2=P−1A2P and (A⊤)2=(A2)⊤.

By (c), every x splits as Ax+(I−A)x with the first part in C(A) and the second in N(A). So Rn=C(A)⊕N(A), and A projects onto C(A) along N(A). The projection is orthogonal exactly when the two subspaces are orthogonal, which happens iff A is symmetric (Exercise (B1)).

Theorem 1.9.2 (Symmetric idempotent matrices)

Let A be an n×n symmetric matrix of rank r. Then A is idempotent iff all its eigenvalues are 0 or 1. In that case

  • (a) A=Q1Q1⊤, where the n×r matrix Q1 has orthonormal columns spanning C(A);

  • (b) rank(A)=tr(A)=r, the multiplicity of the eigenvalue 1;

  • (c) A and I−A are nonnegative definite, and x⊤Ax=‖Ax‖2≤‖x‖2.

Proof. If A is idempotent its eigenvalues are 0 or 1 by Proposition 1.9.1(a). Conversely, if A=QΛQ⊤ with each λi∈{0,1}, then A2=QΛ2Q⊤=QΛQ⊤=A. In that case, order the eigenvalues so that the r ones come first and let Q1 hold the corresponding eigenvectors. Then A=QΛQ⊤=Q1Q1⊤, and C(A)=C(Q1) by Corollary 1.7.3(a). This is (a). The trace is the sum of the eigenvalues, which is (b). For (c), the eigenvalues of A and of I−A are nonnegative, and x⊤Ax=x⊤A⊤Ax. Also ‖x‖2−‖Ax‖2=x⊤(I−A)x≥0.

Chapter 6 shows that symmetric idempotent matrices are exactly the orthogonal projections, and Q1Q1⊤ in (a) is the projection onto C(A). In Chapter 4, part (b) becomes the degrees of freedom of a chi-squared distribution.

Theorem 1.9.3 (Sums of symmetric idempotent matrices)

Let A1,…,Ak be symmetric n×n matrices and A=∑iAi. Consider

  • (i) A is idempotent;

  • (ii) every Ai is idempotent;

  • (iii) AiAj=0 for all i≠j.

Any two of these imply the third. Moreover, if A=In and ∑irank(Ai)=n, then (ii) and (iii) hold.

Proof.

  • (ii) and (iii) imply (i): A2=∑iAi2+∑i≠jAiAj=∑iAi=A.

  • (iii) and (ii) imply (iii): By Theorem 1.9.2(c), ‖x‖2≥x⊤Ax for all x. Fix j and take x=Ajy, so that Ajx=x and x⊤Ajx=‖x‖2. Then ‖x‖2≥x⊤Ax=‖x‖2+∑i≠jx⊤Aix=‖x‖2+∑i≠j‖Aix‖2. So AiAjy=Aix=0 for every i≠j and every y.

  • (iv) and (iii) imply (ii): By (iii), AAi=Ai2. Using A2=A, Ai2=AAi=A2Ai=AAi2=Ai3. The eigenvalues of the symmetric Ai therefore satisfy λ2=λ3, so they lie in {0,1}, and Ai is idempotent by Theorem 1.9.2.

For the last statement, let ri=rank(Ai). For each i, I−Ai=∑j≠iAj has rank at most ∑j≠irj=n−ri (Proposition 1.2.3(d)). So the eigenvalue 1 of Ai, whose eigenspace is N(I−Ai), has multiplicity at least ri. But Ai has exactly ri nonzero eigenvalues, so all of them equal 1 and Ai is idempotent. Now (i) holds trivially, and (iii) follows from the second part of the proof.

The last statement is the algebraic core of Cochran’s theorem (Chapter 4): if y⊤y=∑iy⊤Aiy and the ranks add up to n, the quadratic forms are built from mutually orthogonal projections.

1.9.1. Exercises

A. Check your understanding

Exercise (A1)

Let A,B be idempotent of the same size. Show that AB is idempotent if AB=BA, and that A+B is idempotent iff AB+BA=0. Show that the latter forces AB=0 (symmetry is not needed).

B. Practice

Exercise (B1)

Let A be idempotent. Show that A is symmetric iff C(A)⊥N(A).

Solution. Suppose A is symmetric. For u=Ax∈C(A) and v∈N(A), u⊤v=x⊤Av=0. Conversely, suppose C(A)⊥N(A). For all x,y, (I−A)y∈N(A), so x⊤A⊤(I−A)y=0. Hence A⊤(I−A)=0, that is, A⊤=A⊤A. The right side is symmetric, so A⊤ is symmetric, and so is A.
