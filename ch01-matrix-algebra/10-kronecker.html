1.10  Kronecker products and the vec operator

Balanced designs have model matrices with a product structure, and multivariate observations have covariance matrices with one. The Kronecker product expresses both.

Definition 1.10.1

The Kronecker product of an m×n matrix A and a p×q matrix B is the mp×nq block matrix A⊗B=(aijB). The vec operator places the n columns of an m×n matrix one below another, giving a vector of length mn: vec(A)=(a1⊤,…,an⊤)⊤.

Proposition 1.10.1

For matrices of compatible sizes:

  • (a) (A⊗B)(C⊗D)=AC⊗BD (mixed-product rule);

  • (b) (A⊗B)⊤=A⊤⊗B⊤, and (A⊗B)−1=A−1⊗B−1 for nonsingular A,B. More generally A−⊗B− is a generalized inverse of A⊗B;

  • (c) tr(A⊗B)=tr(A)tr(B) and rank(A⊗B)=rank(A)rank(B);

  • (d) if A (m×m) and B (p×p) are symmetric with eigenvalues λi and μj, the eigenvalues of A⊗B are the products λiμj, and det(A⊗B)=(detA)p(detB)m;

  • (e) Kronecker products of orthogonal, symmetric, idempotent, or nonnegative definite matrices are of the same kind;

  • (f) vec(ABC)=(C⊤⊗A)vec(B) and tr(A⊤B)=vec(A)⊤vec(B).

Proof.

  • (a) The (i,j) block of the left side is ∑kaikckjBD, which is the (i,j) block of the right side. (b) Transposition is direct, and the rest follows from (a), for example (A⊗B)(A−⊗B−)(A⊗B)=AA−A⊗BB−B.
  • (b) With A=PΛP⊤ and B=QMQ⊤, (a) and (b) give A⊗B=(P⊗Q)(Λ⊗M)(P⊗Q)⊤, a spectral decomposition because P⊗Q is orthogonal and Λ⊗M is diagonal with entries λiμj. The determinant is the product of these. (c) The trace statement is direct. For the rank, by (a) (A⊗B)(A−⊗B−)=AA−⊗BB− is idempotent with the same rank as A⊗B (Proposition 1.3.3(a)). Its trace is tr(AA−)tr(BB−)=rank(A)rank(B) by Proposition 1.9.1(b).
  • (c) follows from (a), (b) and (d). (f) The jth column of ABC is ABcj=∑kckjAbk, which is the jth block of (C⊤⊗A)vec(B). The trace identity is ∑ijaijbij written two ways.

The determinant formula in (d) holds for all square A and B, not only symmetric ones. We need only the symmetric case.

Example (The balanced two-way layout)

Let observations yij, i=1,…,a, j=1,…,b, be stacked with j running inside i. The additive model E(yij)=μ+αi+βj has model matrix X=[1a⊗1b, Ia⊗1b, 1a⊗Ib], because Ia⊗1b has a 1 in column i exactly for the b observations in row i. With J¯k=k−1Jk, a symmetric idempotent averaging matrix, the mixed-product rule shows that Ia⊗J¯b, J¯a⊗Ib and J¯a⊗J¯b replace each observation by its row mean, its column mean and the grand mean. The matrix M=Ia⊗J¯b+J¯a⊗Ib−J¯a⊗J¯b is symmetric and idempotent, with trace a+b−1=rank(X). It is the projection onto C(X) (Theorem 16.1.2 in Chapter 16). The script checks this for a=3 and b=4, together with each part of Proposition 1.10.1.

import numpy as np
rng = np.random.default_rng(11)
def vec(M):
    return M.reshape(-1, order="F")                     # stack the columns

A, B, C = rng.normal(size=(3, 4)), rng.normal(size=(4, 2)), rng.normal(size=(2, 5))
assert np.allclose(vec(A @ B @ C), np.kron(C.T, A) @ vec(B))          # vec(ABC)

# balanced two-way layout: a = 3 rows, b = 4 columns, one observation per cell
a, b = 3, 4
one = lambda k: np.ones((k, 1))
X = np.hstack([np.kron(one(a), one(b)),        # intercept
               np.kron(np.eye(a), one(b)),     # row indicators
               np.kron(one(a), np.eye(b))])    # column indicators
print("rank(X) =", np.linalg.matrix_rank(X), "= a + b - 1 =", a + b - 1)

1.10.1. Exercises

A. Check your understanding

Exercise (A1)

With observations stacked as in Example (The balanced two-way layout), the row and column indicator matrices are R=Ia⊗1b and C=1a⊗Ib. Use the mixed-product rule to compute R⊤R, C⊤C and R⊤C, and identify the cross-product matrix X⊤X of the balanced additive model.

Solution. By Proposition 1.10.1(a) and (b), R⊤R=(Ia⊤Ia)⊗(1b⊤1b)=bIa and C⊤C=(1a⊤1a)⊗Ib=aIb, while R⊤C=(Ia⊤1a)⊗(1b⊤Ib)=1a1b⊤, the a×b matrix of ones. With 1ab=1a⊗1b=R1a=C1b, the cross-product matrix of X=[1ab,R,C] is X⊤X=(abb1a⊤a1b⊤b1abIa1a1b⊤a1b1b1a⊤aIb). Every cell of the layout is filled once, so each pair of a row level and a column level occurs exactly once, which is what the block of ones records.

Exercise (A2)

Show that (A⊗B)⊤(A⊗B)=(A⊤A)⊗(B⊤B), and deduce that the Kronecker product of two matrices with orthonormal columns again has orthonormal columns. Show also that ‖A‖F=‖vec(A)‖, so that vec turns the Frobenius norm into the Euclidean norm.

Exercise (A3)

Let Σ=Σ1⊗Σ2 with Σ1 (m×m) and Σ2 (p×p) positive definite — a separable covariance matrix, one variance structure across rows and another across columns. Show that Σ is positive definite, that Σ−1=Σ1−1⊗Σ2−1, that Σ1/2=Σ11/2⊗Σ21/2 in the sense of Theorem 1.7.6, and that log⁡detΣ=plog⁡detΣ1+mlog⁡detΣ2.

B. Practice

Exercise (B1)

Let Y be n×q, let X be n×p with full column rank and let B be p×q, so that q responses are regressed on the same X. Using Proposition 1.10.1(f), show that vec(XB)=(Iq⊗X)vec(B) and that minimizing ‖Y−XB‖F2 leads to (Iq⊗X⊤X)vec(B)=vec(X⊤Y). Deduce that a multivariate regression on a common model matrix is q separate univariate regressions, one per column of Y.

Solution. Part (f) with XBIq gives vec(XB)=(Iq⊤⊗X)vec(B). By Exercise (A2), ‖Y−XB‖F2=‖vec(Y)−(Iq⊗X)vec(B)‖2, an ordinary least squares criterion with model matrix Iq⊗X. Its normal equations are (Iq⊗X)⊤(Iq⊗X)vec(B)=(Iq⊗X)⊤vec(Y), and the mixed-product rule turns the left side into (Iq⊗X⊤X)vec(B) and the right side into vec(X⊤Y). The matrix Iq⊗X⊤X is block diagonal with identical blocks, so the system separates into X⊤Xbj=X⊤yj for each column yj of Y. Nothing is gained by treating the responses jointly, as long as they share a model matrix and the criterion is the unweighted sum of squares.

Exercise (B2)

Show that A⊗B=0 iff A=0 or B=0, and that if A⊗B=C⊗D with A,C of one size and B,D of another, all four nonzero, then C=c−1A and D=cB for some scalar c≠0. A separable covariance matrix therefore determines its two factors only up to reciprocal scalars.

Solution. The (i,j) block of A⊗B is aijB, so the product is zero iff every aijB=0. If A⊗B=C⊗D, then aijB=cijD for all i,j. Choose k,l with akl≠0. If ckl=0 then B=0, which is excluded, so D=cB with c=akl/ckl≠0. Substituting back, aijB=ccijB for all i,j, and B≠0 forces aij=ccij, that is, C=c−1A. Conversely (c−1A)⊗(cB)=A⊗B.

Exercise (B3)

Write J¯k=k−1Jk and Qk=Ik−J¯k, both symmetric idempotent, of ranks 1 and k−1. Expanding Iab=(J¯a+Qa)⊗(J¯b+Qb), show that the four matrices J¯a⊗J¯b,Qa⊗J¯b,J¯a⊗Qb,Qa⊗Qb are symmetric idempotent, sum to Iab and multiply to zero in pairs, with ranks 1, a−1, b−1 and (a−1)(b−1). Show that the matrix M of Example (The balanced two-way layout) is the sum of the first three, so that I−M=Qa⊗Qb. These are the grand mean, row, column and interaction parts of a two-way analysis of variance, and their ranks are its degrees of freedom.

Solution. Each of the four is a Kronecker product of symmetric idempotent matrices, hence symmetric idempotent by Proposition 1.10.1(e), with rank the product of the ranks by Proposition 1.10.1(c). Expanding the product of the two decompositions of the identity gives Ia⊗Ib as their sum. Any two of them have a factor J¯Q=0 or QJ¯=0 in one of the two slots, so their product vanishes by the mixed-product rule. The four ranks add to 1+(a−1)+(b−1)+(a−1)(b−1)=ab, as Theorem 1.9.3 requires. Finally M=Ia⊗J¯b+J¯a⊗Ib−J¯a⊗J¯b=(J¯a+Qa)⊗J¯b+J¯a⊗(J¯b+Qb)−J¯a⊗J¯b, which collects into J¯a⊗J¯b+Qa⊗J¯b+J¯a⊗Qb, of rank 1+(a−1)+(b−1)=a+b−1. Subtracting from Iab leaves Qa⊗Qb.

C. Going deeper

Exercise (C1)

Show that for each m,n there is exactly one mn×mn matrix Km,n with Km,nvec(A)=vec(A⊤) for every m×n matrix A, that it is a permutation matrix, and that Km,n⊤=Km,n−1=Kn,m. Then show that for A of size m×n and B of size p×q, Kp,m(A⊗B)=(B⊗A)Kq,n. This commutation matrix is how the two orders of a Kronecker product are related, and how derivatives with respect to a matrix are rearranged.

Solution. The map vec(A)↦vec(A⊤) permutes the mn coordinates: the entry aij, which sits in position i+(j−1)m of vec(A), sits in position j+(i−1)n of vec(A⊤). A linear map on Rmn is determined by its values on the coordinate vectors, and every coordinate vector is vec(eiej⊤) for some i,j, so Km,n exists and is unique, and it is the permutation matrix just described. Applying the rule twice returns vec(A), so Kn,mKm,n=I; a permutation matrix is orthogonal, so its inverse is its transpose.

For the last identity, let C be q×n, so that both sides may be applied to vec(C). By Proposition 1.10.1(f), (A⊗B)vec(C)=vec(BCA⊤), a p×m matrix, so the left side sends vec(C) to vec((BCA⊤)⊤)=vec(AC⊤B⊤). The right side sends it to (B⊗A)vec(C⊤)=vec(AC⊤B⊤) by the same rule. The two matrices agree on every vec(C), that is, on all of Rnq, so they are equal.

Exercise (C2)

In a balanced two-way layout with random row and column effects, the response vector has covariance V=σα2(Ia⊗Jb)+σβ2(Ja⊗Ib)+σ2Iab,σ2>0. Using the four idempotent matrices of Exercise (B3), show that V=(bσα2+aσβ2+σ2)J¯a⊗J¯b+(bσα2+σ2)Qa⊗J¯b+(aσβ2+σ2)J¯a⊗Qb+σ2Qa⊗Qb, read off the eigenvalues of V with their multiplicities, and write down detV and V−1. On which subspace does V act as σ2I?

Solution. Since Jk=kJ¯k and Ik=J¯k+Qk, Ia⊗Jb=b(J¯a⊗J¯b+Qa⊗J¯b),Ja⊗Ib=a(J¯a⊗J¯b+J¯a⊗Qb), and Iab is the sum of all four. Collecting coefficients gives the displayed form. The four matrices are symmetric idempotent with pairwise products zero and sum Iab, so by Theorem 1.9.2(a) the columns of an orthonormal basis of each column space are eigenvectors of V, with eigenvalue the coefficient in front. The eigenvalues are therefore bσα2+aσβ2+σ2 once, bσα2+σ2 with multiplicity a−1, aσβ2+σ2 with multiplicity b−1 and σ2 with multiplicity (a−1)(b−1). The determinant is their product, detV=(bσα2+aσβ2+σ2)(bσα2+σ2)a−1(aσβ2+σ2)b−1(σ2)(a−1)(b−1), and the inverse replaces each coefficient in the displayed sum by its reciprocal, since the four terms multiply to zero in pairs and each is idempotent. Finally Vu=σ2u for every u∈C(Qa⊗Qb): on the interaction space the row and column variance components have no effect at all, which is why that space supplies the error term of the balanced two-way analysis of variance.
