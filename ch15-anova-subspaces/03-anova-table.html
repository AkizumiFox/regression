15.3  The analysis of variance table from projections

Whether the treatments differ at all is answered by the F test, with the analysis of variance table as its bookkeeping. Both come from one orthogonal decomposition of the observation space into the constants, the treatment space and the error space, and every entry of the table is a property of one piece.

15.3.1. Three subspaces

Write P0=n−111⊤ for the projection onto the constants and M for the projection onto C(Z) (Eq. 15.1.1). Since 1∈C(Z), Theorem 6.5.1 applies to the pair span(1)⊆C(Z) and gives the orthogonal decomposition

Rn=span(1)⏟dim⁡1⊕T⏟dim⁡g−1⊕C(Z)⊥⏟dim⁡n−g,I=P0+(M−P0)+(I−M),
(15.3.1)

with the treatment space T=C(Z)∩1⊥ of Section 15.2 and PT=M−P0. The three projections act on the data in the familiar way: (P0y)kj=y¯,(PTy)kj=y¯k−y¯,((I−M)y)kj=ykj−y¯k. So every observation is split as ykj=y¯+(y¯k−y¯)+(ykj−y¯k), grand mean plus treatment deviation plus residual, and the three vectors so formed are mutually orthogonal. Pythagoras gives

∑k,jykj2=ny¯2+∑knk(y¯k−y¯)2+∑k,j(ykj−y¯k)2.
(15.3.2)

The middle term is the between-groups sum of squares SSB=‖PTy‖2, and the last is the within-groups or residual sum of squares SSE=‖(I−M)y‖2 of Eq. 15.1.2. Their sum ∑k,j(ykj−y¯)2 is the corrected total sum of squares. The same decomposition, with the distributions attached, was the first application of Cochran’s theorem (Example (The one-way layout)).

15.3.2. The F test

Theorem 15.3.1 (The one-way F test)

In the normal one-way model of Definition 15.1.1, with mean vector 𝛉𝛍θ=Zμ, put MSB=SSBg−1,s2=MSE=SSEn−g,F=MSBMSE.

  • (a) Y¯, SSB=∑knk(Y¯k−Y¯)2 and SSE=∑k,j(Ykj−Y¯k)2 are mutually independent.

  • (b) SSE/σ2∼χ2(n−g), and SSB/σ2∼χ2(g−1,γ) with

    𝛉γ=‖PTθ‖2σ2=1σ2∑k=1gnk(μk−μ¯)2,μ¯=1n∑k=1gnkμk.
    (15.3.3)

  • (c) F∼F(g−1,n−g,γ), and γ=0 iff μ1=⋯=μg. In particular, when the means are equal, F∼F(g−1,n−g) whatever their common value and σ2.

  • (d) F is the statistic of the F test of the reduced model E(Y)∈span(1) against C(Z), and of the testable hypothesis α1=⋯=αg in the effects form. The test that rejects when F>Fα(g−1,n−g) is the likelihood ratio test of equal means. Its power is a continuous, strictly increasing function of γ, equal to α at γ=0.

Proof. By Eq. 15.3.1 the matrices P0, PT and I−M are symmetric, idempotent and mutually orthogonal, with ranks 1, g−1 and n−g. Theorem 4.4.4 gives (a), since Y¯ is a function of P0Y, and it gives the chi-squared laws of (b) with noncentralities 𝛉‖PTθ‖2/σ2 and 𝛉‖(I−M)θ‖2/σ2. The second is zero because 𝛉θ∈C(Z). The vector 𝛉θ has entry μk in group k, so 𝛉𝛉Mθ=θ and 𝛉P0θ=μ¯1, and 𝛉PTθ has entry μk−μ¯ in group k. Its squared length is ∑knk(μk−μ¯)2, which proves Eq. 15.3.3.

For (c): by (a) and (b), F is a ratio of independent chi-squared variables divided by their degrees of freedom, the numerator noncentral, which is the noncentral F law (Definition 4.1.2). Since every nk≥1, γ=0 iff μk=μ¯ for every k.

For (d), take X0=1 inside X=Z. Then SSE0−SSE=‖(M−P0)Y‖2=SSB by Eq. 6.5.2, with r−r0=g−1, so F is the statistic of Theorem 11.1.1. In the effects form the hypothesis is αk−αg=0, k<g: g−1 estimable, linearly independent contrasts, so it is testable (Definition 11.3.1). The mean vectors it allows are the constant vectors, so by Theorem 11.3.2(c) its F statistic is the same one. The likelihood ratio property is Theorem 11.4.1, and the power statement is Theorem 11.5.1(a).

The noncentrality Eq. 15.3.3 is a weighted sum of squared deviations of the group means from their weighted mean. The weights are the group sizes, because a group of nk observations carries nk copies of its mean in 𝛉θ. In a balanced layout γ=m∑k(μk−μ¯)2/σ2, the case studied for planning in Proposition 11.5.2 and Example (How many observations per group?). The dependence on the group sizes in unbalanced layouts is taken up in Section 15.5.

15.3.3. Expected mean squares

The distributions in Theorem 15.3.1 need normality. The expectations of the mean squares do not, and they explain why the ratio of two mean squares is the right statistic.

Theorem 15.3.2 (Expected mean squares in the one-way layout)

In the one-way model of Definition 15.1.1 (uncorrelated errors with common variance σ2, not necessarily normal):

  • (a) E(MSE)=σ2;

  • (b) E(MSB)=σ2+1g−1∑k=1gnk(μk−μ¯)2;

  • (c) in the effects form, ∑knk(μk−μ¯)2=∑knk(αk−α¯)2 with α¯=n−1∑knkαk, which is ∑knkαk2 under the side condition ∑knkαk=0. In a balanced layout it is m∑k(μk−μ~)2 with μ~=g−1∑kμk, which is m∑kαk2 under ∑kαk=0;

  • (d) E(MSB)≥E(MSE), with equality iff μ1=⋯=μg.

Proof. The spaces of Eq. 15.3.1 form an analysis of variance decomposition of C(Z) in the sense of Theorem 9.6.1, which gives 𝛉E(SSE)=σ2(n−g)+‖(I−M)θ‖2=σ2(n−g) and 𝛉E(SSB)=σ2(g−1)+‖PTθ‖2. The squared length was computed in the proof of Theorem 15.3.1. Dividing by the degrees of freedom gives (a) and (b). For (c), μk=μ+αk and μ¯=μ+α¯, so μk−μ¯=αk−α¯. In a balanced layout μ¯=μ~ and α¯=g−1∑kαk. (d) follows from (a) and (b). Part (b) is also Exercise (A2), which computes it directly from the group means.

Both mean squares estimate σ2 when the treatments do not differ; otherwise MSB estimates σ2 plus the treatment variation per degree of freedom. Every F ratio in an analysis of variance follows this logic: the denominator’s expectation equals the numerator’s exactly when the hypothesis holds (Section 9.6).

The same projections serve the random-effects model of Section 15.1, but the expectations change. With Cov(Y)=σ2I+σa2ZZ⊤ and a constant mean, E(MSE)=σ2 still, while E(MSB)=σ2+n0σa2 with n0=(n−∑knk2/n)/(g−1), which is m in a balanced layout (Exercise (C1)). The ratio F then tests σa2=0, and the difference of the two mean squares divided by n0 estimates σa2. Chapter 32 develops this.

15.3.4. The table, derived

Collecting the pieces gives the one-way analysis of variance table. Each row is a subspace, its degrees of freedom are the dimension, its sum of squares is the squared length of the projection of y, and its expected mean square is σ2 plus the squared length of the projection of 𝛉θ per dimension.

Source Subspace df Sum of squares Mean square F Expected mean square
treatments T g−1 ∑knk(y¯k−y¯)2 MSB MSB/MSE σ2+∑knk(μk−μ¯)2g−1
error C(Z)⊥ n−g ∑k,j(ykj−y¯k)2 MSE σ2
corrected total 1⊥ n−1 ∑k,j(ykj−y¯)2

Some tables add a row for the mean, with 1 degree of freedom and sum of squares ny¯2=‖P0y‖2, and an uncorrected total ∑ykj2; its F ratio would test μ¯=0, rarely of interest.

Hand computation used the group totals Tk and grand total T: ‖My‖2=∑kTk2/nk and ‖P0y‖2=T2/n, the “correction factor”, so SSB=∑kTk2/nk−T2/n. Such differences of nearly equal numbers lose accuracy in floating point (Chapter 10); computing deviations first, as the projections do, avoids this.

Example (The analysis of variance for the nitrogen trial)

For the thirty yields of Section 15.1, with grand mean y¯=5.6930 t/ha:

Source df Sum of squares Mean square F
nitrogen rates 4 28.7074 7.1768 26.43
plots within rates 25 6.7887 0.2715
corrected total 29 35.4960

The mean row would add ny¯2=972.307 on one degree of freedom. The critical value is F0.05(4,25)=2.759, and the p-value is 1.2×10−8. The table says only that the treatment component of the mean is not zero; the contrasts of Section 15.2 and Section 15.4 say what it is. Since the data were simulated, the true noncentrality is known: γ=103.4, far more than needed for power near one.

import numpy as np
from scipy import stats

rates = np.array([0, 40, 80, 120, 160])            # kg N per hectare
g, m = len(rates), 6                                # 5 rates, 6 plots per rate
true_means = 6.8 - 2.7 * np.exp(-0.018 * rates)     # unknown to the analyst
rng = np.random.default_rng(1515)
group = np.repeat(np.arange(g), m)                  # plot i received rate group[i]
y = np.round(true_means[group] + rng.normal(0, 0.5, g * m), 2)   # yield, t/ha
n = len(y)
Z = (group[:, None] == np.arange(g)).astype(float)  # cell-means model matrix
n_k = Z.sum(axis=0)

M = Z @ np.diag(1 / n_k) @ Z.T
P0 = np.full((n, n), 1 / n)                         # projection onto span(1)
ss_mean = y @ P0 @ y                                # n * ybar^2
ss_between = y @ (M - P0) @ y                       # sum_k n_k (ybar_k - ybar)^2
ss_within = y @ (np.eye(n) - M) @ y                 # sum_kj (y_kj - ybar_k)^2
df_between, df_within = g - 1, n - g
F = (ss_between / df_between) / (ss_within / df_within)
p_value = stats.f.sf(F, df_between, df_within)
print(f"{'source':10s} {'df':>3s} {'SS':>9s} {'MS':>8s}")
print(f"{'rates':10s} {df_between:3d} {ss_between:9.4f} {ss_between / df_between:8.4f}")
print(f"{'plots':10s} {df_within:3d} {ss_within:9.4f} {ss_within / df_within:8.4f}")
print(f"{'corrected':10s} {n - 1:3d} {ss_between + ss_within:9.4f}")
print(f"F = {F:.2f} on ({df_between}, {df_within}) df, p = {p_value:.2g}")

15.3.5. Three ways to read F

As a proportion of variation. Let η2=SSB/(SSB+SSE), the fraction of the corrected total sum of squares that lies in the treatment space. It is the R2 of the regression of y on the group indicators, and the sample version of the correlation ratio of Eq. 14.2.1. By Theorem 9.5.2, F=η2/(g−1)(1−η2)/(n−g), and in the normal model, under equal means, η2 has the Beta law of that theorem, with E(η2)=(g−1)/(n−1), not small with many groups and few observations. In the nitrogen trial η2=0.809: four fifths of the variation among plots is variation among rates.

As the largest contrast. By Proposition 15.2.1(d), every contrast has SS(c)≤SSB, with equality for ck∝nk(y¯k−y¯). Dividing by s2, the largest squared t statistic over all contrasts is (g−1)F. Hence the F test rejects iff some contrast, perhaps not an interesting one, is declared nonzero by Scheffé’s method (Corollary 13.2.3).

As a two-sample test, for g=2. Then T is a line, the only contrast is μ1−μ2 up to scale, and F=T2 for the pooled two-sample t statistic of Exercise (B2), whose sensitivity to unequal variances the one-way test inherits (Section 15.5).

15.3.6. Exercises

A. Check your understanding

Exercise (A1)

A one-way experiment with four treatments and six observations per treatment gives a treatment mean square of 12.0 and a corrected total sum of squares of 96.0. Complete the analysis of variance table, compute F and η2, and state the reference distribution of F under equal means.

Solution. The degrees of freedom are 3, 20 and 23. The treatment sum of squares is 3×12=36, so the error sum of squares is 96−36=60 and MSE=3. Then F=12/3=4, η2=36/96=0.375, and under equal means F∼F(3,20).

Exercise (A2)

Show from Eq. 15.3.2 that SSB=∑kTk2/nk−T2/n and SSE=∑k,jykj2−∑kTk2/nk.

B. Practice

Exercise (B1)

For g=2 with n1+n2=n fixed, show that γ=(μ1−μ2)2n1n2/(nσ2), and that it is largest for n1=n2. By how much does the noncentrality fall for the split n1=n/4, n2=3n/4?

Solution. With μ¯=(n1μ1+n2μ2)/n, μ1−μ¯=n2(μ1−μ2)/n and μ2−μ¯=−n1(μ1−μ2)/n. So σ2γ=(μ1−μ2)2(n1n22+n2n12)/n2=(μ1−μ2)2n1n2/n. The product n1n2 with fixed sum is largest at n1=n2=n/2, where it is n2/4. For the split (n/4,3n/4) it is 3n2/16, so the noncentrality falls by a quarter.

Exercise (B2)

In the normal model with equal means E(η2)=(g−1)/(n−1), which is not zero, so η2 overstates the treatment variation in small experiments. Show that the numerator of ω^2=SSB−(g−1)MSESSB+SSE has expectation ∑knk(μk−μ¯)2, whatever the means, so that it removes the bias from the numerator. When is ω^2 negative?

Solution. By Theorem 15.3.2, E(SSB)=(g−1)σ2+∑knk(μk−μ¯)2 and E{(g−1)MSE}=(g−1)σ2, so the difference has the stated expectation. The numerator is negative iff MSB<MSE, that is, iff F<1, which often has null probability above one half: 0.57 for the nitrogen design’s F(4,25) in the normal model. The ratio itself is not unbiased, since its denominator is random.

Exercise (B3)

In the nitrogen trial, compute the Scheffé-maximal contrast ck=nk(y¯k−y¯) from the rate means, and verify that its squared t statistic is 4F. Describe in words what it compares.

C. Going deeper

Exercise (C1)

In the random-effects model Cov(Y)=σ2I+σa2ZZ⊤, E(Y)=μ1, use Theorem 2.3.1 to show that E(SSB)=(g−1)σ2+(n−∑knk2/n)σa2 and E(SSE)=(n−g)σ2. Show that n0=(n−∑knk2/n)/(g−1)≤n/g, with equality iff the layout is balanced.

Solution. By Theorem 2.3.1, 𝛉𝛉E(Y⊤AY)=tr(AΣ)+θ⊤Aθ, and 𝛉θ=μ1 is annihilated by both PT and I−M. Now tr(PT)=g−1 and tr(PTZZ⊤)=tr(Z⊤MZ)−tr(Z⊤P0Z). Since MZ=Z, the first term is tr(Z⊤Z)=n. The second is n−1‖Z⊤1‖2=∑knk2/n. For the error, (I−M)Z=0, so only σ2tr(I−M)=(n−g)σ2 remains. Finally ∑knk2≥n2/g by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, with equality iff all nk=n/g, so (g−1)n0≤n−n/g=(g−1)n/g.

Exercise (C2)

Show that F is unchanged when y is replaced by ay+b1 with a≠0, and when the observations are permuted within groups. Show that it changes when a constant is added to the observations of one group only, and explain why it should.
